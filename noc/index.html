NOC: first line of defense

You usually see a problem first. Your job is to notice it, confirm it, scope it, communicate it, and get the right people moving fast. You can also take a few safe, pre-approved actions when owners are slow. You are not expected to find the root cause.

  • Detect: run the health sweep every shift. Most services have no dashboard, so the sweep is your dashboard.
  • Triage in 15 minutes: is it real, which layer, which feature, how bad? Then page through PagerDuty.
  • Escalate on a timer. If CFTs don't respond, climb the ladder and use pre-approved actions.
  • Hand off cleanly to SRE with what you saw, checked, and did.

When to hand off to SRE

Page SRE on-call (PagerDuty "Nimbus SRE") when any of these is true:

  • User impact is confirmed and above the normal baselines for more than 10 minutes.
  • More than one feature is affected, or the problem is at a shared layer (Front Door, APIM, auth, Foundry).
  • You've spent 15 minutes and still can't tell which layer or owner is involved.
  • A CFT hasn't acknowledged within 15 minutes.
  • You've taken a pre-approved action. SRE should know, even if it worked.
What SRE needs from you · a good handoff saves 15 minutes

Include

  • What you saw, with numbers and the start time.
  • Scope: feature, region, client version, layer.
  • What you checked, with links to the queries.
  • What you did, and when.

Avoid

  • "Something's wrong with Nimbus." Be specific.
  • Paging without opening the channel and pinned record.

Shift rhythm

Start of shift

  • Read the previous shift's handoff note and any open #inc- channels.
  • Check that PagerDuty shows you on call and that your phone gets a test notification.
  • Run the shift health sweep and note anything unusual.
  • Check scheduled changes: client rollouts in progress, CFT deploys, Azure maintenance.
  • Open the Teams channels for the Collab and Device CFTs, so you can bridge quickly.

During the shift

  • Re-run the sweep every 2 hours, and right after any rollout or deploy you hear about.
  • Acknowledge every page within 5 minutes, even if it's only to say you're looking.
  • Keep notes as you go. They become the handoff.

End of shift

  • Open incidents: current status, IC, next update time.
  • Anything odd that didn't become an incident, and the queries you used.
  • Actions you took that are still in effect (for example, the Proactive Tips batch is still paused).
  • Alerts that were noisy or confusing, for the runbook backlog.

What good looks like

Fast acknowledgement

Pages acknowledged within 5 minutes.

Fast, specific escalation

SRE or the owner paged within 15 minutes of a confirmed impact, with a complete handoff.

Clean records

Channel opened, pinned record started, and timeline kept from the first minute.

Runbook feedback

Every confusing alert is noted, so the next shift has an easier time.
