Coordinate the Incident

Once an incident is declared, the NOC is the Incident Coordinator (IC). You don't debug and you don't change production. You keep the people, the cadence, the decisions, and the record in order, across two chat platforms, slow CFTs, a powerful Cloud Infrastructure team, and leaders who sometimes want to see the raw details.

  • Run the IC loop every 10 to 15 minutes: impact, workstreams, silence, decisions, update.
  • Assign Technical Lead (SRE), bridge, scribe, and comms buffer early.
  • Chase CFTs on the ladder timers. Bring the senior manager in for decisions and stuck escalations.
  • Absorb executive interruptions so responders can keep working.
  • Close with a Jira ticket, a runbook owner, and a scheduled postmortem.

The IC Loop

  1. What's the impact now?Ask the Technical Lead for the user-facing number, or re-run the sweep query yourself.
  2. What are we trying, and who's on it?Each workstream has one name next to it.
  3. Who haven't we heard from?Check the ladder timers for every paged team and every open action request.
  4. Is any decision waiting?Get it made: by the Tech Lead (technical), the owner (their system), or the senior manager (business).
  5. Update.Post the Slack update, mirror it to Teams through the bridge, and update the pinned record.

Running Across Slack and Teams

  • Decisions only count in Slack. Anything agreed in a Teams call gets posted in Slack, prefixed with [from Teams].
  • Join the Teams call if a Teams-only CFT is doing most of the work, and keep a bridge person on Slack.
  • Use the Slack workflows for declaring and the timeline. They don't exist in Teams.
  • Templates: updates and the Teams mirror.

Working With Cloud Infrastructure

Cloud Infrastructure owns the shared layers and has a lot of influence. When they're involved:

  • Lead with evidence. Share the query and the numbers from the Tech Lead, for example "edge 5xx at 4% since 14:05, origin connection errors", not "your CDN is broken".
  • Ask for specific actions through their PagerDuty, and respect their change process. Ask what they need from us to move faster.
  • If they say "not us", ask what evidence would change their mind, and get the Tech Lead to produce it. Escalate through the senior manager, never around them.

Handling Executive Interruptions

Leaders under pressure sometimes dive into the details: they ask for raw queries, message responders directly, open side calls, or ask for root cause while users are still affected. (At Nimbus, see Morgan in The People.) Each interruption costs minutes. Handling this politely and firmly is part of coordinating.

  1. Name a comms buffer early.For Sev 1 it's the senior manager. For Sev 2 it's you. Announce it: "Questions about status go to @buffer, so responders can focus."
  2. Redirect, don't ignore.Acknowledge the question, point to the next update, and make sure it gets answered there.
  3. Pull side calls back.If a separate call or Teams thread starts, ask that its decisions be posted in Slack, and invite the starter into the main channel.
  4. Give them a job.Executives are most useful making decisions: spend, priorities, contacting other leaders.
  5. Escalate kindly.If the interruptions continue, ask the senior manager to step in privately.

Scripts You Can Paste

"Thanks. Good question. The next update is at 14:30, and I'll make sure it answers that."

"The Tech Lead is mid-investigation, so I'll take questions for them. What do you need to know?"

"Could we keep decisions in #inc-… so everyone's working from the same plan? I'll summarize the Teams call there."

"We could use your help with one decision: approving spillover capacity, about $X per hour. Can you make that call?"

Record interruptions neutrally in the timeline. They're a legitimate postmortem topic, framed as a system issue (no exec update format, no comms buffer), never as a person.

Closing the Incident

  • The Technical Lead confirms impact has been at baseline for 30 minutes. Post the resolution update in Slack, mirror it to Teams, and resolve in PagerDuty.
  • Temporary changes still in effect have an owner and an undo time.
  • File the Jira ticket within 24 hours, using the pinned record (how).
  • Schedule the postmortem before standing down (required for Sev 1–2), and invite every CFT involved, Teams users included. Use the postmortem process.
  • Hand the timeline to the postmortem author.
  • Assign a runbook owner if no playbook fit.
