What Good Looks Like

The same incident can go very differently depending on how people respond. Pick a level and a role to see what Weak, Good, and Best in Class look like across nine dimensions of incident response.

  • Weak is where many new teams start: unclear ownership, no cadence, debugging before mitigating, and no learning afterwards.
  • Good is the target for most teams: clear roles, timed escalation, reversible mitigations, and blameless postmortems.
  • Best in Class is when the system does the work: tooling, agreements, and practice make good behavior the default.
Applies Anywhere

These descriptions are written generically, so use them as a self-assessment for any team, as a rubric for tabletop exercises, or to grade a postmortem. Each card ends with a short Nimbus example.

SRE: Escalates by complaining instead of with evidence.

SRE: Common mitigations are rehearsed and documented in runbooks.
Manager: Pre-approves categories of spend so the team doesn't have to ask mid-incident.

How to Use This

Self-Assessment

For each dimension, ask honestly where your team is today. Pick one dimension to move up a level this quarter.

Tabletop Rubric

The tabletop kit scores the debrief on these dimensions.

Postmortem Lens

Ask which dimensions were Weak in this incident, and what action would move them to Good.

Onboarding

New responders read the Good column for their role in week 1.
