Operating Rhythm

Reliability is managed between incidents, not during them. A steady rhythm of short, well-asked reviews (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly) catches problems early, grows your team, and gives executives a picture they can trust.

  • Daily (5 min): scan the NOC handoff for triage threads and open actions.
  • Weekly (30 min): latency and error review by layer, plus the triage-thread review with the NOC.
  • Monthly (45 min): health check, postmortem actions, NOC and SRE growth.
  • Quarterly: executive reliability report, and partner and capacity reviews.

Daily: Five-Minute Check

  • Read the NOC shift handoff. Any triage threads still open? Any requests waiting on owners?
  • Any incident needing an executive SITREP or follow-up today?
  • Anything trending toward a limit (capacity, partner rate limits, error budget)?

Weekly: Latency & Error Review by Layer

A 30-minute review with the SRE lead and a NOC representative. Use LAYERS in review mode (Assess, Yesterday, Evidence, Sustain) and go layer by layer.

MinutesAgendaYour questions
0–10P50 / P95 and error rate per feature vs last week and the SLO"Which layer's latency or errors moved most? Why?"
10–15AI capacity and quota: tokens per minute at peak, any 429s"How close to the limit are we at peak? What's the next launch going to add?"
15–20Device and partners: fallback rate, partner errors and latency"Any partner trending worse? Any client version with a falling local share?"
20–25Triage-thread review with the NOC"Which should have been incidents? What would have helped decide faster?"
25–30Open postmortem actions and runbooks"What's blocked, and what do you need from me?"

Send executives the three-to-five-line weekly reliability note afterwards.

Monthly: Health Check

QuestionWhy it matters
"Are we meeting SLOs for the top features? How much error budget is left?"The clearest view of reliability from the user's side.
"How were incidents detected: alerts, the NOC sweep, or customers?"Customer-detected incidents mean we can't see our own product.
"Time from first signal to declare? Any missed declarations?"Measures the NOC's Triage-or-Incident judgment.
"How long did CFTs and Cloud Infrastructure take to act on requests?"Slow response is a known delay, and the numbers come from pinned records.
"Where is each NOC member on the competency ladder?"Tracks NOC growth (ladder).
"How many postmortem actions are done, and how many runbooks were written?"Shows whether we're learning.
"What share of features have a dashboard and an SLO?"Under 10% today. Track it upward.

Quarterly: Report Upward and Outward

Executive Reliability Report

SLO attainment by feature, the top 3 incidents and what changed, partner reliability, capacity outlook, and the investments needed. BLUF first.

Partner Reliability Review

For each partner: availability, latency, incidents, SLA breaches, and open asks. Raise repeat problems with the partner's account team.

Capacity Review

AI capacity at peak vs limits, upcoming launches, the fallback surge headroom, and the cost outlook.

People Review

NOC level-ups, SRE growth, on-call load, and tooling progress (for example, NOC executable actions).

Red Flags

Act When You See These
  • Incidents with no coordinator, or two parallel channels.
  • The same incident repeating because actions weren't done.
  • Late declarations: triage threads that sat for an hour before becoming Sev 1–2.
  • One engineer is the only person who can diagnose a feature or a partner integration.
  • The NOC or engineers are afraid to escalate.
  • A partner breaching its SLA with no conversation about it.
  • Teams arguing about whose problem it is while users are affected.
