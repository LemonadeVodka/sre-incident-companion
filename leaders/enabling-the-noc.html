Enabling the NOC

Our NOC is offshore, works in a second language for many, and is still building technical depth. They also run every declared incident. That combination fails if we expect expert judgment, and works well if we give them clear scripts, simple decisions, plain English, and safety to escalate. Your questions are the main tool for growing them.

  • Replace judgment calls with scripts and yes/no questions, such as the Triage or Incident? check.
  • Use plain English: short sentences, no idioms, one idea per line, numbers instead of adjectives.
  • Grow them up a competency ladder: Observe → Compare → Locate → Escalate Well → Suggest.
  • Protect one rule above all: nobody is criticized for declaring or escalating.

Principles

Scripts Over Judgment

Turn every recurring decision into a checklist or yes/no question. Judgment grows from repetition, not from being thrown in.

Plain English

Short sentences. No idioms ("ballpark", "on fire"). Numbers, not "a lot". Templates they can fill in.

See the Layers

Put the layer stack on the wall. "Which layer?" is easier with a picture.

Safe to Escalate

Early escalation is praised, always. A NOC afraid of being wrong escalates late, and late is expensive.

Explain the Why

When you redirect, explain why in one line. That's how rules turn into understanding.

Respect the Time Zone

They often handle the first hour alone. Make sure the runbooks, contacts, and PagerDuty routes work without anyone awake on our side.

Coaching "Triage or Incident?"

The NOC's hardest call is often whether something is an incident at all. Give them the three-state check (Watch, Triage, Incident) and its four questions: Users? Size and time? Spread? Need help?, with a 15-minute triage time box. Then:

  • Run a weekly 15-minute review of triage threads: which should have been incidents, and which were noise.
  • Praise every early declaration in public, even the ones that turned out to be nothing.
  • Track missed declarations (a triage that became Sev 1–2 late) and time from first signal to declare.
  • When a call was wrong, ask "What would have helped you decide?" Never ask "Why didn't you?"

Measuring Progress

  • Time from first signal to declare: trending down.
  • First page to the right owner: above 80%.
  • Handoff completeness: SRE follow-up questions per incident trending down.
  • Missed declarations: rare, and each one reviewed blamelessly.
  • Runbooks contributed by the NOC: rising.
