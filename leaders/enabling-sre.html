Enabling SRE Engineers

Your best tool for developing engineers is the question that makes them think one level deeper than they would on their own. You're not checking their work. You're raising the technical bar of the conversation, and for junior engineers, giving them the steps to climb it.

  • Use question ladders: start where the engineer is, then climb one rung at a time.
  • Learn the AI-specific prompts (tokens, first token, capacity, fallback, retries, model versions, partners). They spark the most useful thinking.
  • "I don't know" is a good answer if it's followed by "and here's how I'd find out".

The Question Ladder

Climb one rung at a time. A junior engineer may start at rung 1. A senior engineer should be comfortable at rungs 4 and 5.

  1. Show Me"Can you show me the chart you're looking at?" This grounds the conversation in data.
  2. What's Normal?"What does this look like on a normal day? Yesterday at this time?"
  3. Where Is It?"Which layer is adding the time or the errors? How would you prove it's that layer?"
  4. What Else?"What else could explain the same chart? What's the cheapest way to rule it out?"
  5. What Would You Change?"If you could change the design so this can't happen, or so we'd see it in a minute, what would it be?"

AI-Specific Technical Prompts

Latency

  • "Is first-token time up, or generation time? What does each tell us?"
  • "Did input or output tokens grow? From a prompt change, retrieval chunks, or a model version?"
  • "How close to capacity (PTU / tokens per minute) are we at peak?"
  • "Is P50 moving, or only P95? Who's in the slow 5%?"

Error Rates

  • "Which layer returned it? Edge, gateway, ours, partner, or model?"
  • "Are we counting user-visible failures, or retried successes too?"
  • "Is it 429 (capacity) or 5xx (broken)? Different owners, different fixes."
  • "Are retries making it worse? How many calls per request now vs normal?"

Device & Routing

  • "What's the local vs cloud split by client version? Did the fallback rate change?"
  • "Is fallback rate part of the rollout health gate?"
  • "Can the cloud absorb a 30% fallback surge? Where's that written down?"

Partners

  • "Is it the partner or our connector? How do we tell?"
  • "Are our timeouts to the partner shorter than our own latency budget?"
  • "Do we have a circuit breaker and a degraded mode per partner? Can we turn off one connector alone?"
  • "Which partner SLA applies, and are we tracking it?"

Resilience & Design

  • "What's the blast radius if this layer fails? Which features go down with it?"
  • "Are background and interactive workloads isolated?"
  • "What's the most reversible mitigation? How fast can we undo it?"

Visibility

  • "What can't we see right now? What would it take to see it?"
  • "Is sampling hiding the tail?"
  • "Which alert would have caught this 10 minutes earlier?"

When They Say "I Don't Know"

Don't

Show disappointment, answer for them, or move on to someone more senior. They'll stop saying "I don't know" and start guessing.

Do

"Good, that's honest. How would you find out?" Then wait. If they're stuck, offer the next rung of the ladder.

Best

Come back later: "What did you find?" Close the loop, and the learning sticks.

Protecting Their Focus

During incidents your engineers are the Technical Lead. Ask your questions through the coordinator, in the channel, at update times. Keep executives and other leaders off their DMs. Save the deep "why" questions for the postmortem.
