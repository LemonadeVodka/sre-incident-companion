Jargon Decoder

Keep this open during incidents. The left column is what engineers say, the middle is what it means, and the right is why you should care.

Error Codes

They sayIt meansWhy you care
"401s"The system didn't accept the user's or service's credentialsUsers may be effectively locked out. Often caused by a sign-in change or an expired secret.
"403s" / "WAF blocks"The request was recognized but refused, often by our security firewallA security rule may be blocking real users.
"429s" / "throttling"We hit our usage limit with the AI providerFeatures fail until load drops or we buy more capacity. Usually a cost or priority decision for you.
"5xx" / "500s" / "503s"Something on the server side brokeWhich layer matters: ours, Microsoft's, or the CDN's.
"502 / 504 at the edge"The CDN couldn't reach our systems, or they answered too slowlyOften affects everyone at once.
"Timeouts"Something took so long that the system gave upUsually a slowness problem underneath.

Speed and Reliability

They sayIt meansWhy you care
"Latency"How long users waitSlow AI answers are our most common incident.
"P50"The typical wait: half of users wait less than thisThe everyday experience.
"P95"How long the slowest 1 in 20 users waitsThe bad-day experience. If P95 doubles, many users notice.
"Error rate"The share of requests that fail1% sounds small, but at our volume it's a lot of people.
"Time to first token"How long until the AI starts showing its answerThis is what makes Nimbus feel fast or slow.
"SLO"Our reliability target, for example "99.5% of requests succeed"A business decision. You help set it.
"Error budget"How much failure the target allows before we must slow down feature workWhen it's used up, reliability work takes priority.
"Burn rate"How fast we're using the error budgetA high burn rate means it's urgent.

Systems and Capacity

They sayIt meansWhy you care
"CDN" / "Front Door" / "edge"The internet-facing entrance in front of all our servicesWhen it fails, everything fails.
"APIM" / "gateway"The checkpoint that verifies credentials and routes requestsSits in front of every feature.
"Foundry" / "the model"Microsoft's AI service that generates the answersShared by every team. Some problems are on Microsoft's side.
"PTU" / "provisioned capacity"AI capacity we reserve and pay for whether we use it or notPredictable but fixed. Overflow costs extra.
"Spillover" / "pay-as-you-go"Sending overflow to on-demand AI capacityKeeps features working, at extra cost.
"Noisy neighbor"One team's workload using up shared capacityA priority and governance question.
"Failover"Switching to a backup regionRestores service, but may affect data residency and cost.
"Rollback"Undoing a recent changeUsually the fastest fix. It may delay a launch.
"Staged rollout" / "ramp"Releasing a new app version to a growing share of usersPausing it limits the damage from a bad release.
"Panic" (Rust)The program hit something unexpected and stoppedUsually triggered by a recent change. Rollback is typical.
"Telemetry" / "traces"The data our systems report about themselvesIf it's missing, we're flying blind.
"KQL"The query language engineers use to search that dataYou don't need it. Ask for the chart instead.

Layers and AI Terms

They sayIt meansWhy you care
"Which layer?"Which part of the request path: device, edge, gateway, orchestrator, feature, partner, or modelThe layer tells you the owner and the right questions. See Know the Layers.
"Orchestrator"The part that decides where a request goes: on the device (local vs cloud), or in the cloud (which feature)Orchestrators sit in front of everything. When they fail, many features fail together.
"On-device" / "local model"A small AI model on the user's PC that answers simple requestsFast and free, but if it fails the cloud absorbs the work.
"Fallback surge"Local answers failing, so the work moves to the cloudCauses cloud slowness and 429s with no server-side change.
"Tokens"Units of text the AI reads and writesMore tokens means slower, more expensive, and quota used up faster.
"Partner integration" / "connector"Our link to a third-party service (Zoom, Microsoft 365, OEM APIs)Partner outages break features. We contain them, and can't fix them.
"Circuit breaker" / "kill switch"Automatically or manually stopping calls to a failing dependencyKeeps one bad partner or service from dragging everything down.
"Triage thread"A NOC note for "maybe a problem", time-boxed to 15 minutesThe step before declaring. When in doubt, declare.

Reporting Terms

TermMeaning
BLUFBottom Line Up Front: the one sentence an executive needs first.
SITREPSituation report: situation, impact, cause, actions, decisions needed, next update.
AARAfter Action Review: what was supposed to happen, what happened, why the difference, and what we sustain or improve.
LAYERSOur question framework: Locate, Assess, Yesterday, Evidence, Response, Sustain.

Process Words

They sayIt means
"IC"Incident Commander: the person running the response
"Sev 1 / Sev 2"Severity: Sev 1 is most users affected, Sev 2 is a major feature affected
"Mitigated"Users are no longer badly affected, but the cause may not be fixed yet
"Resolved"Stable and back to normal
"Bridge person"Someone relaying updates between Slack and Teams
"Postmortem"A blameless review after an incident, to learn and prevent a repeat
"Runbook" / "playbook"Written steps for handling a known problem. We have few so far.
"Toil"Repetitive manual work that could be automated
