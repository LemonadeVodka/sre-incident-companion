Updating Executives

Executives need three things: what it means for customers and the business, how confident we are, and whether they need to decide anything. Give them that, briefly and on a schedule, and they stop reaching past you into the incident.

  • Lead with the BLUF (bottom line up front): one sentence on impact, status, and any decision needed.
  • Follow with a SITREP: Situation, Impact, Cause (with confidence), Actions, Decisions Needed, Next Update.
  • Translate metrics into user terms. Never send a raw chart without a sentence explaining it.
  • Say how confident you are, honestly: known, likely, or unknown.

Confidence Language

Known

"We have confirmed…" Use only when the data shows it and the fix proved it.

Likely

"We believe… and are confirming by [time]." The honest default mid-incident.

Unknown

"We don't yet know the cause. Our leading theory is X, and we'll update at [time]." Better than false certainty.

When Executives Dive In

Some leaders, under pressure from above, dive into the details: they ask for raw queries, message engineers directly, or open side calls. (At Nimbus, this is Morgan, the director in our background story.) Your job is to be the comms buffer:

  • Get ahead of them with a SITREP before they ask.
  • Offer a direct line: "I'll update you every 30 minutes. The team needs to stay heads-down."
  • Give them a real job: a decision, or an escalation to another org's leadership or a partner's executive.
  • Afterwards, raise it neutrally in the postmortem as a process gap (no exec update format), never as a person.

"I'll be your single point of contact. You'll get a SITREP from me every 30 minutes. Could you help us get the partner's VP on the phone? That's our biggest blocker right now."
