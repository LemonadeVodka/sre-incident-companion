Incident Management Playbook

In an incident, your NOC coordinates, your SREs lead the technical work, and the owning teams make the changes. Your job is to make sure the response is working: ask the questions that sharpen it, make the decisions only you can make, keep executives informed, and keep everyone else out of the way.

  • When in doubt, declare. Downgrading costs one message. A missed incident costs trust.
  • Ask, don't take over. Use LAYERS: Locate, Assess, Yesterday, Evidence, Response, Sustain.
  • Own the upward story. Send SITREPs on a cadence, with the bottom line first.
  • Practice monthly with a tabletop, so the real thing feels familiar.

  2. Stand up the response (NOC as IC, SRE as Tech Lead)
  3. Lead without diving in
  4. Ask LAYERS questions
  5. Update executives with a SITREP
  6. Use the scenario runbooks

It's Working WhenTime from first signal to declare is under 15 minutes. The NOC detects most incidents before customers do. SITREPs go out on cadence. No missed declarations turn up in review.
Weak

Late declaration, the manager debugs, executives hear rumors first.

Good

Declared early, clear IC and Tech Lead, SITREPs on cadence, the manager decides and unblocks.

Best

The NOC runs it confidently, executives trust the cadence, and every incident improves a runbook.

Your Jobs in an Incident

1. Ask the Right Questions

LAYERS, at the right layer, for latency and errors. Make the team think, without doing their work for them.

2. Make the Calls Only You Can

Spend, kill switches, failover, customer messaging, and pulling in people.

3. Update Executives

BLUF and SITREP: impact in business terms, honest confidence, and the decisions you need.

4. Be the Buffer

Keep other leaders, including your director, from pulling responders off the fix.
