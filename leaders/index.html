The senior manager path

This track is written for leaders, not engineers. It has no query language and explains every term. It covers how Nimbus works, where the business risk is, and what to do, ask, and decide before, during, and after incidents.

  • Your role in incidents: make sure someone is in charge, make the business decisions, unblock other teams, and protect responders.
  • Between incidents: fund the fixes for our known gaps (monitoring, runbooks, tooling, CFT on-call).
  • Read in order. The whole path takes about 30 minutes.

Nimbus in two minutes

Nimbus is an AI assistant that comes installed on Windows PCs. People use it to search their files, rewrite text, recap meetings, and fix device problems. The app on the PC talks to our cloud services, which call an AI model hosted on Microsoft Azure (Azure AI Foundry).

A request passes through several layers, and any of them can fail:

LayerPlain-English jobWhen it fails, users see…Owner
Desktop appWhat the user sees on their PCCrashes, "can't connect"Client team
Sign-in (Entra ID)Proves the user or device is allowed in"Please sign in again", features refusing to workPlatform
Front door / CDNThe internet-facing entrance that protects and speeds up trafficEverything fails at oncePlatform
Gateway (APIM)Checks credentials and routes each request to the right team's serviceErrors across many featuresPlatform
Feature servicesEach team's code for its featureOne feature broken or slowThat feature's team (CFT)
AI model (Foundry)Generates the answers. Capacity is shared by all teams.Slow answers or "busy, try later" across featuresPlatform

Five CFTs (cross-functional teams) each own one feature. A Platform team owns the shared layers. The NOC watches around the clock and raises the alarm, and SRE on-call investigates and runs incidents.

Where the business risk is

Shared AI capacity

Every feature draws on the same AI capacity. If one team's background job uses too much, customer-facing features slow down or fail.

Many layers, few eyes

Fewer than 10% of our services have monitoring dashboards. Diagnosis depends on a handful of people writing queries by hand.

New team, few runbooks

Most incidents are handled from scratch, so response depends on who's on call.

Slow team response

Some CFTs have no on-call rotation, and two work in Teams while our incident tooling is in Slack. Minutes are lost finding people.

Weak record-keeping

Jira is locked down, so incident history and follow-ups are hard to track and easy to lose.

Sign-in and edge failures

A single change to sign-in or the CDN can block every user at once. These layers need careful change control.
