Playbook 1 · Incident Management

Scenarios in Plain English

These are our ten most likely incidents, without the jargon. Each one tells you what's happening, what users feel, the typical fix and how long it takes, the decisions you may be asked to make, and the questions to ask. Engineers use the matching playbooks.

  • Most of our incidents are slowness, failures, CDN problems, or sign-in rejections.
  • Your decisions usually involve money (more capacity), priority (which feature to protect), risk (rollback, failover), or communication.

One Feature Is Slow

What's happening
Users of one feature (say, Writing Assist) wait much longer than usual. Other features are fine.
What users feel
"Nimbus is slow when I rewrite text." Some give up and stop using the feature.
Typical fix
Usually a recent change by that team, such as a new release or a bigger AI prompt. A rollback typically fixes it within 30–60 minutes.
You may decide
Approve a rollback even if it delays that team's launch.
Ask
Did something change recently? Can we roll it back? How many users are affected?
LAYERS
Layer: Feature Services
Ask the NOC: Which feature is slow? Did this team deploy today?
Ask SRE: Which hop grew (model, retrieval, our code)? Did prompts get bigger?
Tell executives: "[Feature] is slower after a recent change. The team is rolling it back."

We Hit Our AI Usage Limit

What's happening
Requests are rejected because we're over our capacity with the AI provider ("429s"). This often happens when one team's background job uses up capacity everyone shares.
What users feel
"Nimbus is busy, try again later." Customer-facing features fail while background tasks run.
Typical fix
Pause the background workload, or buy overflow capacity. Usually under 30 minutes once someone decides.
You may decide
Which feature gets priority, and whether to approve the extra spend for overflow capacity.
Ask
Whose traffic is using the capacity? Can it pause? What does overflow cost per hour?
LAYERS
Layer: Model Provider (capacity)
Ask the NOC: Is it 429? Which deployment? Is a background job running?
Ask SRE: Who is using the quota (tokens per minute by caller)? Is it a retry storm?
Tell executives: "We hit our AI capacity limit. We've paused background work and may need overflow capacity at about $X per hour."

The AI Provider Is Having Problems

What's happening
Microsoft's AI service is failing or slow for many features at once.
What users feel
Many features fail or hang. It looks like "Nimbus is down".
Typical fix
Switch to our backup region, and open a Microsoft support case. Anywhere from 15 minutes to several hours, depending on Microsoft.
You may decide
Approve failover (check data residency and cost), notify executives, and approve customer messaging.
Ask
Is it on Microsoft's side? Is a support case open, and at what severity? Can we use the other region?
LAYERS
Layer: Model Provider
Ask the NOC: Is Azure status green? Is it one region or all?
Ask SRE: One deployment or all? Can we fail over, and with what capacity?
Tell executives: "Our AI provider is having problems. We're moving traffic to our backup region and have opened a case."

Answers Take Long to Start

What's happening
The AI takes longer than usual to begin its answer, even if the full answer then arrives normally.
What users feel
Nimbus feels sluggish, with the spinner staying up longer before any text appears.
Typical fix
Free up capacity, or shrink the request. Often within an hour.
You may decide
Whether to accept slightly lower answer quality (a smaller model, less context) for speed, temporarily.
Ask
Is it capacity or how we build the request? How noticeable is it to users?
LAYERS
Layer: Model Provider / Feature
Ask the NOC: Which feature feels slow to start? Since when?
Ask SRE: First token or generation? Capacity queueing or prompt size?
Tell executives: "Answers are slower to start because of [capacity / bigger requests]. Fix expected within [time]."

A New App Version Is Misbehaving

What's happening
Problems are limited to users who received the latest desktop app update.
What users feel
Crashes or errors right after updating. Support tickets mention "since the update".
Typical fix
Pause the rollout immediately (the NOC can do this), then the team fixes the bug. Pausing takes minutes. The fix takes days.
You may decide
Hold the release, and coordinate messaging with release management, partners, and support.
Ask
Has the rollout been paused? What share of users have the new version?
LAYERS
Layer: Device
Ask the NOC: Is it only the newest app version? Has the halt been requested?
Ask SRE: What changed in the build: API, retries, token refresh?
Tell executives: "A new app update is causing errors for [X%] of users. The rollout is paused."

Users Complain but Our Monitoring Says Fine

What's happening
Support tickets or social posts say Nimbus is broken, but our monitoring shows nothing wrong.
What users feel
Whatever they're reporting. The problem is that we can't see it.
Typical fix
First the team has to measure the problem. Time varies, and it often reveals a monitoring gap.
You may decide
Treat "customers told us before our monitoring did" as a serious finding, and fund the fix.
Ask
How do we know how many users are affected? Why didn't our monitoring catch it?
LAYERS
Layer: Telemetry / Device
Ask the NOC: Did data volume drop? Collect 3–5 user reports.
Ask SRE: What part of the path is invisible to us right now?
Tell executives: "Customers noticed a problem before our monitoring did. We're measuring the impact and closing that gap."

The Front Door (CDN) Is Failing

What's happening
The internet-facing entrance to our services is failing to reach them, or our security firewall is blocking requests.
What users feel
Often everything fails at once: "Nimbus can't connect". Or one group of users is blocked.
Typical fix
Cloud Infrastructure fixes the connection, reverts a firewall rule, or reroutes traffic. 15–60 minutes is typical. Some causes are on Microsoft's side.
You may decide
Approve rerouting or rollback of a security rule (security sign-off), and approve customer messaging, since impact is usually broad.
Ask
Is it all users or some regions? Did anyone change a firewall or CDN setting? Is it Microsoft's edge?
LAYERS
Layer: Edge / CDN
Ask the NOC: Is the edge 5xx up? All regions? Are our services quiet?
Ask SRE: Edge or origin? What does the error info say? Any WAF or CDN change?
Tell executives: "Many users can't connect because of our network entry point. We're rolling back a recent change."

Users or Services Are Being Refused at Sign-in

What's happening
The system is rejecting credentials ("401s"). This can be all users (a server-side sign-in change), one app version (a bug), or our own services (an expired secret).
What users feel
Features refuse to work, or "please sign in again" loops. Sometimes total lockout.
Typical fix
Undo the recent sign-in or configuration change, or renew the expired secret. Fast once the cause is found, often under an hour.
You may decide
Approve an emergency change to sign-in settings, and customer messaging if many users are locked out.
Ask
Is it everyone or one app version? Did anything change in sign-in, keys, or certificates? When do our secrets expire?
LAYERS
Layer: Gateway & Auth
Ask the NOC: All APIs or one? One app version or all?
Ask SRE: Which layer rejected the token? What changed or expired?
Tell executives: "Some users are being asked to sign in again. We've found the configuration change and are reverting it."

Devices Suddenly Lean on the Cloud

What's happening
Some requests are normally answered on the PC itself. A faulty update to the on-device AI model or a driver makes that fail, so devices quietly send the work to the cloud. The cloud gets a surge it wasn't sized for.
What users feel
Quick tasks become slower. Then other features slow down or fail as the shared cloud capacity runs out.
Typical fix
Pause the device update (Client CFT) and add cloud capacity or pause background work (Cloud Infrastructure). Under an hour once both teams are engaged.
You may decide
Approve the extra cloud spend, and hold the device release. Make sure the Client CFT and Cloud Infrastructure are working on it together.
Ask
Did a device or AI model update just go out? Are both the device team and the cloud team on the call?
LAYERS
Layer: Device Orchestrator → Model Provider
Ask the NOC: Is a device or model update rolling out? Did the local share drop?
Ask SRE: Fallback rate by local model version? Can the cloud absorb it?
Tell executives: "A device update is pushing extra work to the cloud. We've paused it and added capacity."

A Partner Service Is Down

What's happening
A third-party partner (for example a meeting platform, a productivity suite, or a device maker's support API) is slow or failing. Our systems are fine, but the feature that depends on that partner breaks for its users.
What users feel
For example, Catch Up fails only for Zoom meetings, or Device Care can't check warranty status.
Typical fix
We can't fix the partner. We contain it: disable that one connector, show a friendly message or fallback, and work with the partner. Duration depends on them.
You may decide
Disable the integration temporarily, approve customer messaging that names the partner, and escalate through the partner's executive or account contact if their support is slow.
Ask
Is it the partner or our connector? Does the partner's status page confirm it? Who owns the partner relationship right now? Is this an SLA breach?
LAYERS
Layer: Partner Integrations
Ask the NOC: One partner or all? What does their status page say? Has the partner contact been notified?
Ask SRE: Partner-side or our connector (schema change, token refresh)? Is there a circuit breaker and a per-partner kill switch?
Tell executives: "[Partner] is having an outage that affects [feature] for their users. Our systems are fine. We've disabled the integration and are working with the partner."
