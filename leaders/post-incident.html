Post-Incident Actions

The incident is over for responders when it's resolved. For you, it has just moved into its most important phase. What you do in the next 30 days decides whether the team learns, whether executives trust the process, and whether the same incident happens again.

  • Run an After Action Review with four questions: what was supposed to happen, what happened, why the difference, and what we sustain or improve.
  • Follow the timed checklist: T+0, T+24 hours, T+5 days, T+30 days.
  • Coach the NOC and SRE, send executives a post-incident SITREP, and hold partners and teams to their actions.

The After Action Review

The After Action Review comes from the military, and it's built for teams learning quickly under pressure. Use these four questions to frame the postmortem review you chair:

  1. What Was Supposed to Happen?Our plan, SLO, runbook, or expectation. For example: "The NOC declares within 10 minutes; the owner acknowledges within 15."
  2. What Actually Happened?The timeline from the NOC's pinned record, with times and numbers. Facts only.
  3. Why Was There a Difference?Contributing factors in the system: detection, ownership, tooling, capacity, partner, process. Never a person.
  4. What Will We Sustain and Improve?Name what went well so it keeps happening. Then pick 3–5 owned, dated actions.

Timed Checklist

T+0: At Resolution

  • Thank the responders by name in the incident channel: the NOC coordinator, the SRE Technical Lead, and the CFT and partner contacts.
  • Confirm temporary changes have an owner and an undo time (paused jobs, halted rollouts, disabled partner connectors, extra spend).
  • Send executives a short "resolved" note, with the full SITREP to follow within 24 hours.
  • Check the NOC has scheduled the postmortem and you're the chair (Sev 1–2).

T+24 Hours

  • Send the post-incident SITREP (use the builder and choose "Post-incident"): impact, cause, confidence, prevention, decisions.
  • Make sure the Jira incident ticket exists, and the timeline has been handed to the SRE author.
  • If a partner was involved: request their incident report and root cause, and note the SLA impact.
  • If another team's change contributed (a CFT or Cloud Infrastructure): a calm, factual note to their manager inviting them to the review.

T+5 Days: The Review

  • Chair the review using the AAR questions and the agenda. Open with the blameless statement.
  • Rate the response with Weak / Good / Best, and pick the weakest dimension to improve.
  • Make sure each action has one owner team, a date, and a type (prevent, detect, mitigate, process), and that it's in a tracker.
  • Decide what you'll fund or unblock, and say so in the meeting.

T+30 Days: Follow-Through

  • Review every action: done, on track, or blocked. For blocked ones, unblock, escalate, or explicitly drop with a reason.
  • For done actions, ask "How will we know it worked?"
  • Send executives a one-line follow-up: "X of Y actions complete. Remaining due by [date]."
  • Check whether the playbook or runbook was updated.

Coaching After the Incident

NOC Coordinator

One thing they did well, and one skill to build, in plain English and in writing. Examples: "Your declare message had clear numbers." / "Next time, name the comms buffer at the start." Move them up the competency ladder.

SRE Technical Lead

"What did you learn about this layer?" and "What would make this a 5-minute diagnosis next time?" Ask them to share it with the team.

Yourself

Which decision did you take too long on? Did your questions help, or did they interrupt? Did executives get what they needed before they asked?

Partners and Other Teams

  • Partners: ask for their incident report, track SLA breaches, and raise repeat issues at the quarterly partner review. Ask the team about our own resilience: "Do we have a circuit breaker and a degraded mode for this partner?"
  • Cloud Infrastructure and CFTs: agree joint actions with their managers in the room. Track them on the same list as our own.

Recognition

Recognize behaviors you want repeated: early declaration, a clear handoff, an honest "I don't know", a calm coordinator. Public thanks within a day matters more than a reward weeks later. Teams that are thanked after incidents keep volunteering for on-call.

Applies Anywhere

The After Action Review and the T+0 / T+24 h / T+5 d / T+30 d rhythm work for any team. What you do in the 30 days after the incident matters more than the incident itself.
