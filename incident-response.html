Incident process

How we run incidents across the NOC, SRE on-call, five CFTs, a Platform team, and senior management, from the first alert to the last action item. For how this works with our actual tools (PagerDuty, Slack and Teams, a locked-down Jira), see Ways of working.

  • Six phases: detect, triage, declare, mitigate, resolve, learn.
  • Severity follows user impact. If you're unsure, go higher. Sev 1–2 always get an Incident Commander.
  • The affected CFT owns the incident until the evidence points to shared infrastructure. Mitigate before you debug.
For senior managers · where you fit in the response

Your role

  • You're usually not the IC. You're the escalation point for decisions and resources.
  • Handle executive and customer messaging through the comms lead.
  • Approve costly or risky mitigations quickly.

Questions to ask

  • What severity is this, and why?
  • Is there one channel and one IC, even with several teams involved?
  • Should customers or partners hear from us, and when?
  • Is there a decision waiting on me?

Avoid

  • Downgrading severity to make the numbers look better.
  • Starting a second channel or bridge for executives that pulls responders away.

When in doubt, go higher

Downgrading a Sev 2 to a Sev 3 costs one message. Under-declaring costs hours of lost coordination. Severity can change at any time, and the IC owns that call.

Roles

These roles come from the Incident Command System that firefighters use, which Google's SRE book adapted for software. In a small incident one person holds every role. When the incident grows, hand roles out explicitly.

Incident Commander (IC)

Owns the incident. Coordinates, decides, and delegates, but doesn't debug. Keeps the big picture and sets the severity.

Ops lead

Directs the hands-on investigation and mitigation. The only role that makes changes to production during the incident.

Comms lead

Sends stakeholder updates on schedule and shields responders from "any update?" messages.

Scribe

Keeps the timeline: what was observed, decided, and changed, with timestamps. Writing the postmortem is much easier with a good timeline.

Subject-matter experts

CFT engineers and Platform specialists pulled in by the IC for a specific question.

Coordinating across CFTs

  1. The affected CFT owns it first.If one feature experience is affected, that CFT's on-call is the default IC.
  2. Pull in Platform on evidence, not hunches.Signs it's Platform's problem: several features affected, model dependency latency or errors rising across deployments, 429s on a shared deployment, or gateway errors.
  3. One incident, one channel.When several CFTs notice the same problem, merge into one incident with one IC. Parallel investigations waste effort and send mixed messages.
  4. Hand off out loud.When IC moves from a CFT to Platform, say so in the channel: "Handing IC to @name. Current state: …". No silent handoffs.
  5. Escalate on a timer.No progress in 30 minutes on a Sev 1 or 60 minutes on a Sev 2? Escalate to the next tier or a secondary on-call.
Reality check: unclear owners

If you can't tell who owns a service (no cft.owner, a cryptic cloud_RoleName), don't let that stop you. Page Platform as the default owner and keep mitigating. Then file an ownership gap as a follow-up.

Mitigate before you debug

Your first job is to reduce user impact, not to find the root cause. Most mitigations can be reversed, and an outage that runs on can't be undone. These are the levers you have:

LeverUse whenWhoRisk
Roll back service deployThe problem started after a deployCFTLow
Flip feature flag / revert prompt templateA prompt, model-version, or config change lines up with the startCFTLow
Halt client rolloutOnly a new desktop build is affectedClient CFTLow
Pause background workloads429s or saturation on a shared deploymentInsights CFT / PlatformLow (tips arrive late)
Shift traffic to another deployment or regionOne deployment or region is degradedPlatformMedium (capacity, data residency)
Enable spillover / raise quotaSustained demand over PTU capacityPlatformMedium (cost)
Graceful degradationA dependency such as retrieval is failingCFTMedium (lower-quality answers)
Scale out a serviceService CPU or thread saturationCFTLow

After the incident

  • Write the postmortem draft within 2 business days (required for Sev 1–2).
  • Hold a blameless review with every involved CFT and Platform.
  • Give every action item a single owner and a due date, and track it in the backlog.
  • File observability gaps you hit: missing dashboards, broken traces, misrouted alerts.
  • Update or create the playbook for this scenario.
  • Check that alerts would catch this sooner next time.

Anti-patterns to avoid

The hero

One person debugs silently for an hour without declaring. Declare early, because it's cheap.

The debugging IC

The IC gets lost in a query and coordination stops. The IC delegates.

Root cause first

Users stay broken while you look for the "why". Mitigate first.

Parallel incidents

Three CFTs open three channels for one Foundry problem. Merge them.

Write-only postmortems

Action items never get done. Track them like features.
