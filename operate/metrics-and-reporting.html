Metrics & Reporting

What you measure shapes how your team and your vendor behave, and what your executives believe. Choose a small set of metrics that reflect user experience and operational health, report them consistently, and be ready to explain why some popular ones mislead.

  • User experience first: P50/P95 latency, error rate, and SLO attainment per feature.
  • Operational health second: time to declare, time to mitigate, detection source, action completion, page load.
  • Be careful with MTTR and with counting incidents. Both are easy to game and noisy.
  • Report weekly (3–5 lines), monthly (scorecard), quarterly (exec reliability report).

Metrics That Mislead

MTTR (Mean Time to Resolve)

Incidents vary too much for an average to mean anything, and one long incident skews it. It also encourages closing incidents early. Use time to mitigate, shown as a distribution, instead.

Incident Count

It goes down when people stop declaring. Pair it with missed declarations and detection source.

Uptime %

"99.9% up" can hide a slow, frustrating product. AI products fail through slowness and quality as often as through outages.

Average Latency

It hides the slowest users. Always report P50 and P95.

Tickets Closed (Vendor)

Rewards speed over judgment. Measure outcomes instead.
