Run the Operation

Service Operations Model

Incidents get the attention, but reliability is decided by five connected practices. As the senior manager you own how they fit together, even when other teams own parts of them. This page is your map.

  • Incident (restore service) → Problem (remove the cause) → Change (ship safely) → Capacity (stay ahead of demand) → Vendor & partner (manage what you don't own).
  • Most organizations are good at incidents and weak at problems. Postmortem actions that never close are a problem-management failure.
  • For an AI product, capacity (model quota, fallback surges) and change (prompts, model versions, routing policy) need extra attention.

How They Connect

  1. An incident is declared through Triage or Incident, and run by the NOC and SRE.
  2. The postmortem becomes the entry point to problem management: it produces actions with owners.
  3. Many actions are changes: canaries, rollout gates, capacity isolation. They go through change management.
  4. Capacity findings feed the quarterly capacity review and launch sign-offs.
  5. Vendor and partner findings feed the scorecard, the QBR, and the partner review.

AI-Specific Change Management

In AI products, many risky changes don't look like code deploys. Make sure each one is visible and gated:

Change typeRiskGate to ask for
Prompt template editsToken growth, latency, qualityFlag-controlled, eval suite, token budget check
Model version upgradesOutput length, speed, filter behaviorPinned versions in production, canary, evals
On-device model or driver updatesFallback surge to the cloudFallback rate as a release gate

Change Visibility for the NOC

Ask every team to post significant changes to a shared change feed the NOC watches. "What changed?" (the Y in LAYERS) is the fastest question in any incident, but only if the answer is somewhere to find.
