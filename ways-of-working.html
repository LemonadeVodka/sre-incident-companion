Ways of Working

Our tools aren't ideal yet. Jira is locked down, our workflows are Slack-only while some CFTs live in Teams, not every CFT is in PagerDuty, and we're a new team with few runbooks. This page describes how we run incidents well anyway.

  • PagerDuty pages people. Chat is only for coordination. Never rely on a Slack or Teams message to wake someone up.
  • The Slack incident channel is the source of truth. A named bridge person mirrors it into Teams for Teams-only CFTs.
  • While Jira is locked down, keep the record in the channel plus a pinned record, and file one Jira ticket afterwards.
  • Slow CFT? Follow the escalation ladder with timers, and send pre-agreed requests to whoever can act.
  • Cloud Infrastructure owns the shared cloud and has a lot of influence. Bring evidence, use their process, and escalate through management.
  • New team: every incident leaves behind a runbook, even a rough one.

What This Means for Each Role

RoleYour responsibilities here
NOC (Incident Coordinator)Declare, page through PagerDuty, open the Slack channel and pinned record, act as bridge (or assign one), run the escalation timers, send action requests, file the Jira ticket, and schedule the postmortem. The NOC can't change production.
SRE on-call (Technical Lead)Investigate, recommend mitigations, give owners the evidence to act, and write the technical analysis for the postmortem.
ManagerBe the top rung of the escalation ladder, unblock CFTs that don't respond, and push for the tooling fixes listed at the bottom of this page.

Reality check: CFTs without a PagerDuty service

Not every CFT has its own PagerDuty service yet. For those, page the "Nimbus CFT leads" escalation policy, which reaches the CFT's engineering manager. Write down which CFTs lack a service. That list is one of the manager asks below.

Slack and Teams: One Source of Truth

  1. Slack is the source of truth.Channel name: #inc-YYYYMMDD-short-name, for example #inc-20261004-device-care-429. All decisions and the timeline live here.
  2. Name a bridge person as soon as a Teams-only CFT is involved. It's the NOC by default until the IC reassigns it. The bridge person's only job is relaying: they don't debug.
  3. Open one Teams chat named the same as the Slack channel, with the CFT on-call or lead. If the CFT needs to talk it through, start a Teams meeting and invite the IC.
  4. Mirror every update using the template below, and bring CFT findings back to Slack, prefixed with [from Teams].
  5. Never split decisions. A decision only counts once it's in the Slack channel.

Latest: [one line]

Keeping a Record Without Jira

Our Jira project is locked down: no incident issue type, limited fields, slow permission changes. Don't fight it during an incident.

  • During: pin the incident record (template below) at the top of the Slack channel and keep it updated. The scribe (or IC) owns it.
  • During: use the Slack timeline workflow, or just post HH:MM — what happened messages.
  • Within 24 h: file one Jira ticket in the existing project, using the closest available issue type, titled [INCIDENT] Sev X — short name — date. Paste the pinned record into the description.
  • Action items: create them as normal Jira tasks, linked to the incident ticket.
  • Teams-only CFTs: send them the Jira link and the final pinned record.

When CFTs Don't Respond

Slow responses are a known problem. Treat them as a process issue, not a personal one, and keep the clock running. Don't wait idle: mitigate within your authority while you escalate.

Time since first pageNo acknowledgement? Do thisWho
T+0Page the CFT's PagerDuty service, or the "CFT leads" policy if they have no service. Post in their Slack or Teams channel too.NOC
T+15 minPagerDuty escalates to the CFT secondary. Re-page if needed, and call them if you have a phone number.NOC / SRE
T+30 minSRE pages the CFT engineering manager and notifies the senior manager in the channel.SRE
T+45 minThe senior manager contacts the CFT's director or leadership directly.Senior manager
MeanwhileSend pre-agreed requests to owners who are responding. Cloud Infrastructure can often shift traffic or add capacity even when a feature CFT is silent.NOC (IC) + SRE

Timers run at Sev 1 pace (halve them) or Sev 3 pace (business hours only). Record every page and acknowledgement time in the pinned record, because leadership needs those numbers to fix response expectations.

Working With Cloud Infrastructure

The Cloud Infrastructure CFT owns Front Door, APIM and auth policies, the cloud orchestrator, Foundry capacity and quota, and the telemetry pipeline. Every other team depends on them, they run their own change process, and they're used to getting their way. Working well with them is a core incident skill.

Bring Evidence

A query, a chart, and a time window. "Edge 5xx 4% since 14:05, OriginConnectionError" gets action. "Your CDN is broken" gets defensiveness.

Use Their Front Door

Page their PagerDuty and follow their change process, even under pressure. Ask: "What do you need from us to move faster?"

"Not Us" Isn't the End

Ask what evidence would change their view, and have the Technical Lead produce it. Disagreements escalate through managers, never around them.

When Their Change Caused It

Stay blameless and factual. In the postmortem, focus on how changes to shared layers are reviewed and announced, not on who pushed the button.

Applies Anywhere

Every organization has a powerful platform or infrastructure team. The same rules apply: evidence over opinion, their process over shortcuts, and managers for disagreements.

Building Runbooks as a New Team

We don't have many runbooks yet, and that's fine. The goal is to never handle the same incident from scratch twice.

  1. Every incident leaves a runbook.The IC assigns one person to write it within 3 business days. A rough one is far better than none.
  2. Start from the starter playbooks.Copy the closest playbook and adjust it to what actually happened.
  3. Write for the NOC at 3am.Use specific queries, specific thresholds, and who to page. Avoid "investigate as needed".
  4. Prioritize by pain.Each month, list the top 5 alerts by frequency, and the incidents that took longest to scope. Write those runbooks first.
  5. Test them.A new team member follows the runbook during a quiet shift. Wherever they get stuck, the runbook needs fixing.
MarkdownMinimal runbook templateEnough to be useful; expand later
# [Alert or symptom name]
Owner: [CFT / Cloud Infrastructure]   Last tested: [date]   Severity guide: [..]

## What this means
[1–2 sentences, plain language]

## Check (NOC)
1. [query link] — normal is [X]; incident if [Y]
2. Is it one feature / region / client version?

## Request (NOC → owner)
- [pre-agreed request, who executes it, how to undo it]

## Escalate
- Page [PagerDuty service] — if no ack in 15 min: [next rung]

## Investigate (SRE)
- [likely causes and how to confirm each]

## History
- [date] — [incident link] — [what we learned]

Tooling Asks (For Managers to Drive)

Jira Incident Project

An incident issue type with severity, impact, and timeline fields, plus permission for SRE and NOC to create issues.

Teams Incident Workflow

Mirror the Slack workflow in Teams, or connect PagerDuty to both so incident updates post automatically.

PagerDuty for Every CFT

A service and escalation policy for each feature experience, with a named on-call.

Response Expectations

Agreed acknowledgement times per severity, reviewed monthly using the numbers from pinned records.
