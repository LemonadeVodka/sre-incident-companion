Triage or Incident?

Most late incidents start as "not sure yet". This framework gives your NOC, and you, a fast and defensible way to decide: three states, four yes/no questions, one time box. The rule that makes it work is simple: when in doubt, declare.

  • Watch (log it) → Triage (a thread plus a 15-minute time box) → Incident (declare).
  • Declare if users are affected AND it's above normal for 10+ minutes, OR it's on a shared layer, OR you need help.
  • Still unsure after 15 minutes? Declare. Nobody gets criticized for declaring.

Use It When

A signal looks off but nobody is sure it matters: an alert, a support ticket spike, a sweep anomaly, or a partner status notice. Teach it to the NOC vendor as part of their SOW runbooks, and use it yourself when someone asks "should we call this an incident?"

The Check

Not sure if it's an incident yet? Use three states, four yes/no questions, and one time box. When in doubt, declare. Declaring is cheap, and you can always downgrade.

Watch

Something looks odd, but there's no user impact and it's inside normal ranges.

Do: note it in the shift log and re-check at the next sweep.

Triage

Possibly affecting users, but the cause is unclear.

Do: open a triage thread (not an incident), FYI the likely owner, and time-box 15 minutes.

Incident

User impact is confirmed or likely.

Do: declare and coordinate.

If still unsure then → declare.
  • If a triage becomes an incident, its thread becomes the start of the timeline.
  • Triage threads are reviewed weekly with your manager. Nobody is criticized for declaring.

Your Role as the Manager

  • Make it part of the NOC vendor's required runbooks, and the weekly triage-thread review (how).
  • Praise early declarations in public, even the false alarms.
  • Track missed declarations and time from first signal to declare in the monthly metrics.
