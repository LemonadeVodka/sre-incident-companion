Leading the Technical Response

The NOC runs the incident, and you run the problem. That means organizing the technical work, turning findings into clear recommendations for the people who own the systems, and protecting your own focus from the noise around you.

  • Split the work into named workstreams, and tell the IC who's on each.
  • Give owners recommendations, not orders: what to do, why, the evidence, and how to undo it.
  • Protect your focus. Send status questions and executive DMs to the IC.
  • Mitigate first. Save the root-cause hunt for after recovery and the postmortem.

Workstreams

As soon as more than one person is investigating, split the work and tell the IC:

Recommending Mitigations

You don't execute changes, and neither does the NOC. Owners do. Make it easy for them to say yes:

TextMitigation RecommendationTo the IC, who turns it into an action request
Recommend: [action] by [owner team]
Why: [evidence — query link + numbers]
Expected effect: [..] within ~[N] min
Risk: [low/medium — what could go wrong]
Undo: [how]
If declined: [next best option]
  • Prefer reversible actions: pause, halt, shift, or degrade before you redeploy.
  • One change at a time, so you can tell what worked.
  • Decisions that cost money or quality go to the senior manager through the IC.

Protecting Focus

A leader DMs you: "What query are you running? Why isn't this fixed?" Every answer costs minutes and your train of thought, and there's usually a follow-up. (At Nimbus, see Morgan in The People.)

  • Don't answer DMs mid-investigation. Reply once and move on: "I'm heads-down; @IC has the latest and I'll update them at HH:MM."
  • Share one chart, not raw queries. When leaders need to see something, post a single chart in the channel through the IC.
  • Don't debate hypotheses with spectators. Write them in the channel. Anyone with evidence can add to them.
  • Ask the IC for cover. "@IC can you take questions for a while? I need 15 minutes." That's a normal request.

"I'm heads-down on the fix. @IC has the latest status and I'll brief them at 14:30. Thanks for understanding."

After Recovery

  • Confirm impact has been at baseline for 30 minutes, and tell the IC.
  • Make sure temporary mitigations have an owner and an undo time.
  • Save the queries and charts you used, because they're postmortem evidence.
  • Draft the technical analysis within 2 business days (template).
  • Turn what you learned into a runbook, or improve the playbook you used.
