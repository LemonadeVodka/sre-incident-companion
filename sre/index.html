SRE On-Call: Technical Lead

In a Nimbus incident the NOC coordinates and you lead the technical response: find the failing layer, work out the options, recommend mitigations, and give owners the evidence to act. You're part of a centralized SRE team, not embedded in any CFT, so most of the systems you're debugging belong to someone else.

  • You're the Technical Lead. The NOC is the Incident Coordinator. Let them run the room while you keep your head in the problem.
  • Start with the layer: device, edge, gateway, cloud orchestrator, feature service, or model. It tells you the owner and narrows the causes.
  • You recommend, and owners (CFTs, Cloud Infrastructure) execute. Make your recommendations specific and backed by evidence.
  • After the incident you write the technical analysis for the postmortem.
Applies Anywhere

A technical lead who isn't also coordinating thinks faster and makes fewer mistakes. If you find yourself writing status updates or answering executives, hand that work to the coordinator.

Joining an Incident

  • Acknowledge in PagerDuty and join the #inc- channel.
  • Read the pinned record and the NOC's brief. Ask about anything unclear once, in the channel.
  • Confirm: "I'm Technical Lead. @NOC is IC."
  • Re-run the NOC's query to confirm the impact yourself.
  • Tell the IC your first hypothesis and what you'll check, so they can update stakeholders.

Centralized SRE: How We Work

Nimbus SREs are one central team serving five feature CFTs, the Client CFT, and Cloud Infrastructure. That gives breadth, but it brings some real difficulties:

ChallengeWhat it causesHow we handle it
Thin context on each featureSlower diagnosis in unfamiliar codeService cards for each feature: owner, dashboards, runbooks, dependencies, normal numbers. One page each, kept by the CFT.
No control over CFT backlogs or deploysReliability fixes wait behind features"SRE advises, CFT owns": agreed in writing, with postmortem actions tracked to the CFT and reviewed by managers
Spread across many teamsRelationships are weak when an incident hitsOne SRE liaison per CFT, who attends their planning monthly and knows their on-call
Cloud Infrastructure's influenceHard to push changes to shared layersEvidence-first requests, and shared postmortem actions with their management in the room
Being seen as "the people who get paged"CFTs disengage from reliabilityProduction readiness checks before launches: monitoring, PagerDuty service, runbook, capacity

Your First 10 Minutes

  1. Confirm impact yourself.Numbers drift, so check the NOC's query still holds.
  2. Find the layer.Run the layer comparison, and check the local vs cloud split for device-side causes.
  3. Check for a change.Deploys, client or local model rollouts, routing policy, AzureActivity, prompt or model versions.
  4. Recommend a mitigation if one is obvious.Tell the IC which owner should do it, so they can send the request.
  5. Keep going.Write hypotheses in the channel, and test the cheapest one first.

What Good Looks Like

Weak / Good / Best in Class: SRE as Technical Lead
Weak

Disappears into a query alone, answers executive DMs, argues with owners without data, and the root cause comes before mitigation.

Good

Finds the layer quickly, posts hypotheses, recommends specific reversible mitigations, and gives owners the evidence.

Best in Class

Everything in Good, plus explains reasoning so the NOC learns, protects their focus through the IC, and writes a postmortem analysis that leads to real system changes.
