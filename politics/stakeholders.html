Stakeholders & Influence

Most of what you need (CFTs prioritizing reliability fixes, Cloud Infrastructure isolating capacity, executives funding NOC tooling) depends on people who don't report to you. Influence comes from knowing what each of them needs, and helping them get it while you get what you need.

  • Map every stakeholder on power × interest, and know what they need to win.
  • Pre-wire: meet key people one-on-one before any important meeting.
  • Build coalitions. Three CFTs asking for the same thing beats you asking alone.

Pre-Wiring Decisions

Decisions are rarely made in the meeting. They're made in the conversations before it. For any important proposal:

  1. List who must say yes, and who could say no.Include anyone whose silence in the meeting would hurt you.
  2. Meet each one 1:1 beforehand.Share a short pre-read. Ask "What would make this work for you? What worries you?"
  3. Adjust the proposal.Incorporate their input and credit it: "Building on X's point…"
  4. Close the loop.Tell them what changed, so nobody is surprised in the room.
  5. In the meeting: the decision should feel obvious. If someone pushes back, take it offline.

Use the pre-wire message and decision memo templates.

Influence Without Authority

Reciprocity

Give first: incident support, data for their QBR, a shout-out. People help those who've helped them.

Shared Goals

Frame the ask in their terms: "Isolating background capacity means your feature stops getting throttled at 9 a.m."

Evidence

A chart showing their feature's 429s, or the cost of the last incident. Hard to argue with.

Make It Easy

Bring the draft design, the ticket text, or the SRE who'll pair with them. Lower the cost of saying yes.

Social Proof

"Two other CFTs already adopted the production readiness check."

Escalate Last

Escalation spends capital and relationships. Use it when the user impact justifies it (how).

Building Coalitions

Before asking leadership for something big (NOC executable tooling, separate capacity for background workloads, a Jira incident project), gather allies who share the pain: CFT managers whose features suffered, the support lead who fielded tickets, the vendor whose SLA depends on tooling. Bring their voices, and their data, into the decision memo.
