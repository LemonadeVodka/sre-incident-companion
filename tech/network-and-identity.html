Network & Identity

Before a request ever reaches a model, it crosses the edge, a gateway, private networking, and several identity checks. These layers cause the fastest, broadest outages: the 5xx spike at the edge, the 401 wave after a key rotation, the 403 from a new firewall rule.

  • Find which layer returned the error first. An edge 502 and an orchestrator 502 are completely different problems.
  • 401 = "I don't know who you are" (token missing, expired, or invalid). 403 = "I know you, and the answer is no" (permissions, WAF, policy).
  • Networking changes (DNS, private endpoints, firewall) are a top cause of sudden, total failures.

Edge: CDN and WAF

  • 502/504 at the edge usually means the origin was slow or unreachable. Check the origin first, then the edge.
  • A response timeout at the edge can cut off long AI answers that aren't streamed. A rule of thumb: long generations need streaming or a longer timeout.
  • WAF false positives: AI prompts are long, free text, and sometimes look like attacks to generic rules. A new WAF rule set can block real users with 403s.
Ask: "Are the errors generated at the edge, or passed through from the origin? Any WAF or edge config change in the last 24 hours?"

API Gateway

The gateway validates tokens, applies rate and token limits, routes to backends (including a secondary model region), and can apply circuit breakers. It's also a common place for policy mistakes.

Ask: "Which gateway policy returned this? Did a policy, product, or subscription change?"

Private Networking and DNS

Production services usually reach data stores and model endpoints through private endpoints, which depend on private DNS resolving the service name to a private IP. If DNS is misconfigured, the service resolves to the public address, gets blocked, and fails, often across everything at once.

Classic Pattern

"Everything that talks to X failed at 14:02, and a network change was made at 14:00." Ask for the DNS resolution result from inside the service's network, not from a laptop.

TLS and Certificates

  • Expired certificates cause sudden, total failures at a predictable time. Ask: "Is certificate expiry monitored, with alerts 30 days ahead?"
  • Desktop clients may pin certificates or ship with specific trust stores. Corporate proxies that inspect TLS can break them for enterprise users only, which shows up as a narrow cohort.

Identity and Tokens

User Tokens

The device signs in through an identity provider (Microsoft Entra ID at Nimbus) and gets an access token, typically valid for about an hour, which the client refreshes. Refresh bugs in a client version cause 401s for that version only.

Managed Identity

Cloud services authenticate to each other without stored secrets. A missing role assignment on a new deployment or resource gives a 403.

Keys and Secrets

Rotating a key or secret without updating every consumer gives a wave of 401s starting at the rotation time.

Clock Skew

Tokens have validity windows. A device with a wrong clock can fail validation and look like a 401 cohort.

The Sync Relay

Nimbus syncs context between a user's devices directly when they're on the same network, and otherwise through an encrypted relay service in the cloud. Relay problems show up as Catch Up and Next Step being late or missing on the second device, not as errors on the first.

Ask: "What's the relay connection success rate and sync lag at P95, by region? Is the problem cross-device only?"
Go Deeper
  • Connection pool exhaustion: services keep a limited pool of outbound connections. Slow downstream calls hold connections longer and the pool runs out, causing timeouts unrelated to the downstream error rate.
  • SNAT port exhaustion: heavy outbound traffic from a small set of IPs can run out of ports, causing intermittent connection failures under load.
  • HTTP/2 and streaming: long-lived streaming responses behave differently through proxies and the edge. Test them explicitly.
