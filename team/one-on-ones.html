1:1s, Feedback & Performance

The basics of people management matter more in operations than almost anywhere else. On-call is stressful, incidents expose people, and your director's style adds pressure. Regular 1:1s, clear feedback, and visible growth are what keep good engineers.

  • 1:1s are theirs: their agenda first, yours second, and a career topic monthly.
  • SBI feedback (Situation, Behavior, Impact) for praise and correction. Make it specific, timely, and private.
  • GROW (Goal, Reality, Options, Will) for coaching, instead of giving answers.
  • Underperformance: clear expectations, documented, with support and time. No surprises at review time.

1:1s

WhoCadenceFocus
SREs (US and India)Weekly or biweekly, 30 min, at times that rotate fairlyBlockers, on-call load, growth, morale after hard incidents
Ops leadWeekly, 45 minVendor, process, the 30/60/90 technical plan
Vendor shift lead (NOC)Weekly ops review, not a 1:1Work outcomes only (see co-employment)

Use the 1:1 template. Ask monthly: "How is on-call feeling?" and "Is anything about how leadership is treating the team affecting you?"

SBI Feedback

Situation

When and where. "In yesterday's Sev 2 bridge…"

Behavior

What they did, observable and specific. "…you posted the layer comparison and named the owner within 10 minutes…"

Impact

What it caused. "…which cut our diagnosis time in half and helped me brief the director quickly."

The same structure works for correction: "In the 14:00 update (S), the impact numbers were missing (B), which meant leadership asked three follow-up questions and pulled you off the fix (I). What would make it easier to include them?"

GROW Coaching

  1. Goal"What do you want to get out of this?"
  2. Reality"What's happening now? What have you tried?"
  3. Options"What could you do? What else?" Get at least three before you offer one.
  4. Will"What will you do, and by when? How will I know?"

Career Conversations

Quarterly, separate from status: "Where do you want to be in two years? What skills does that need? What project here would build them?" Write down the answer and revisit it. Use the template.

Underperformance

  1. Check yourself first.Are expectations clear and written? Are they getting the support and training they need? Is something outside work going on?
  2. Name it early and privately.Use SBI, with specific examples, and agree on what "good" looks like.
  3. Agree a plan.Two or three measurable expectations, support offered, and a review date (typically 30–60 days).
  4. Document as you go.Short notes after each check-in. Involve HR early for anything formal.
  5. Follow through.Recognize improvement clearly. If there's none, escalate formally with HR.
Not for Contractors

For NOC contractors, raise performance concerns with the vendor, not with the individual. See co-employment boundaries.
