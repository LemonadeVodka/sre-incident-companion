Morale & Burnout

Operations teams burn out quietly: nights, pressure, blame, and repetitive toil. Add a leader whose style demotivates people, and good engineers leave. Morale is part of your reliability strategy. A burned-out team detects later, decides worse, and quits at the worst time.

  • Watch the early signs: cynicism, silence in channels, fewer volunteers, more sick days, people stopping asking questions.
  • Fix the structural causes: page volume, on-call fairness, toil, unclear ownership.
  • After a demotivating interaction with leadership, run morale repair within 24 hours.
  • Recognize behaviors publicly and specifically, including contract NOC staff, through the vendor.

Early Signs of Burnout

Withdrawal

Quieter in channels and meetings, cameras off, no more "I'll take it".

Cynicism

"Nothing changes." "Why bother writing the postmortem?"

Errors

More mistakes on routine tasks, and missed handoffs.

Avoidance

On-call swaps, more sick days, not volunteering for incidents.

Structural Fixes

CauseFix
Too many pagesMeasure pages per shift. Delete or tune alerts nobody acts on. Target fewer than 2 actionable pages per on-call shift.
Unfair on-callMonthly fairness review (how)
Endless toilCap toil time. Fund automation. Rotate toil across regions.
Blame after incidentsEnforce blameless postmortems and protect them from leadership interruptions.
Never-ending incidentsHand over at shift change, and send people home after long Sev 1s.

Morale Repair After a Demotivating Interaction

When a senior leader publicly criticizes the team, dismisses their work, or dives in harshly during an incident, act within 24 hours:

  1. Acknowledge it.Don't pretend it didn't happen. "That was a tough session yesterday."
  2. Separate signal from tone."Underneath it, the concern was X. That's fair, and here's what we'll do about it." Leave out the harsh delivery.
  3. Reaffirm what went well.Specific, factual credit for the work.
  4. Own the follow-up."I'll handle the conversation with leadership. You focus on the fix."
  5. Check in privately with whoever took the brunt of it.

Never badmouth the leader to the team. It feels supportive, but it erodes trust in the whole leadership chain, including you. Handle the leader through managing up.

Recognition

  • Public, specific, fast: name the behavior and its impact in the team channel within a day.
  • Upward: credit people by name in SITREPs and executive reports.
  • Contract NOC: recognize great work in the channel and in writing to the vendor's account manager, so it reaches their performance process.
  • Both regions: make sure recognition is balanced between the US and India.

Team Rituals

Incident Wins

A monthly five minutes: the best catch, the best handoff, the best "I don't know, but I'll find out".

Learning Hour

Rotating presenters across regions, covering one technical topic or postmortem story.

Pulse Check

A monthly anonymous three-question survey: on-call load, clarity, and feeling supported.
