SREs Across US & India

A follow-the-sun team can give you 24-hour coverage without anyone working nights, or it can quietly split into two teams with different status, work, and morale. The difference is how deliberately you design handoffs, on-call, and who gets the interesting work.

  • One team, two time zones. Same standards, same rituals, same career path.
  • Hand off in writing, every day, with the same template. Overlap time is precious, so spend it on decisions and teaching.
  • Rotate the interesting work. Watch for the "toil goes to one region" pattern.
  • Be fair about on-call, and measure it.

Follow-the-Sun Design

ElementGood practice
CoverageIndia covers its business day (overnight US). The US covers its business day. Each region's on-call is primary in its own daytime, with a secondary from the other region.
HandoffTwice a day, in writing (template), posted in the team channel, with a 15-minute live overlap when possible.
Incident command across the handoffA Technical Lead hands over explicitly (template). Nobody hands over mid-mitigation without a summary.
Overlap hoursProtect 1–2 overlap hours for decisions, design reviews, and pairing. Not for status, which belongs in writing.
The SRE dailyThe one meeting that must be in the overlap: 15 minutes, board walked right to left, facilitator rotating between regions (how).
MeetingsRotate meeting times so the same region isn't always the one up early or late.

Avoid the Two-Tier Team

The Common Failure

Without meaning to, one region gets the alerts, tickets, and toil, and the other gets design work, postmortem authorship, and executive visibility. Within a year you have two teams, unequal careers, and attrition in the region doing the toil.

  • Track who authors postmortems, design docs, and automation, by region, every quarter.
  • Rotate "toil captain" and "project captain" roles across both regions.
  • Give each region ownership of at least one meaningful reliability project.
  • Make sure executives hear from engineers in both regions (demo days, postmortem readouts).
  • Have one promotion bar and one rubric, applied by the same calibration group.

On-Call Fairness

Measure it monthly: pages per person, after-hours pages, and incidents led. Rebalance when one person or region carries more than their share. Use the on-call fairness review template.

Centralized SRE: Giving Them Leverage

Your SREs serve every CFT but own none of their backlogs. Help them with:

  • "SRE advises, CFT owns", agreed in writing with CFT leadership.
  • A named SRE liaison per CFT, spread across both regions.
  • Production readiness checks before launches: monitoring, an on-call owner, a runbook, capacity.
  • You escalating stalled reliability work with CFT leadership, so engineers don't have to fight those battles alone.

Career Paths

Junior → Mid

Leads Sev 3 investigations, writes runbooks, completes the KQL training, and pairs on Sev 2.

Mid → Senior

Technical Lead for Sev 1–2, authors postmortems, runs a game day, and owns a CFT liaison relationship.

Senior → Staff

Drives a cross-team reliability initiative (SLOs, capacity, isolation), mentors across regions, and influences Cloud Infrastructure.

Develop them with the question ladders in Developing SRE Engineers.
