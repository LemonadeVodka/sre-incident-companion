Managing a Contract NOC

Your NOC is fully contracted. That changes how you lead it. You don't manage the people directly. You manage the outcomes, through the vendor: what the contract says, how performance is measured, and how the relationship runs. Get that structure right and the contract NOC can be excellent. Get it wrong and you end up with a demotivated team you can't legally manage and a vendor you can't hold accountable.

  • Lead through three levers: the SOW (what they do), the SLAs and KPIs (how well), and the governance rhythm (weekly ops review, monthly scorecard, quarterly QBR).
  • Respect co-employment boundaries: direct the work and the outcomes, not the people's employment. Confirm the specifics with your HR and procurement.
  • Protect yourself from attrition: runbooks, recorded training, and knowledge that lives with you, not only with the vendor.
  • Give them the shift health sweep (below) and the Triage or Incident? check as required runbooks.

The Three Levers

1. The SOW

The scope of work: monitoring hours, which systems, the incident coordinator role, escalation duties, runbook execution, reporting, language and shift coverage, and named key personnel (shift leads). If it isn't in the SOW, you can't hold them to it.

2. SLAs & KPIs

Measurable targets with consequences (service credits) and improvement plans. Keep them few and meaningful. A short list follows below.

3. Governance

A weekly ops review (your Ops lead plus the vendor shift lead), a monthly scorecard, and a quarterly business review (QBR) with the vendor's account manager.

Avoid Vanity KPIs

"Tickets closed" and "alerts handled" reward speed over judgment. Measure outcomes (declared on time, routed correctly, records complete), not activity.

Co-Employment Boundaries

With contractors, what you direct matters legally as well as operationally. The general principle: the vendor employs and manages the people, and you specify and evaluate the work. Rules vary by country and contract, so confirm with your HR, legal, and procurement teams.

Generally fine: direct the workGenerally the vendor's job: manage the people
Defining runbooks, processes, quality standardsHiring, firing, performance reviews, discipline
Giving feedback on work output to the vendor leadIndividual compensation, promotions, bonuses
Training on your systems and processesSetting individual schedules and leave (beyond coverage requirements)
Including them in incident channels, tabletop exercises, and postmortemsIncluding them in your FTE team events, perks, and internal performance processes
Recognizing great work, via the vendor and in the channelTreating them as your direct reports in org charts and 1:1 performance management
Route Feedback Through the Vendor Lead

Coaching on the work ("the declare message needed numbers") is fine in the moment. Anything about a person's performance or conduct goes to the vendor shift lead or account manager, so they can act on it.

Knowledge Retention Under Attrition

  • Runbooks live in your repository, not the vendor's. The vendor contributes, and you own them.
  • Record the training: walkthroughs of the layers, the sweep, and the Triage-or-Incident check.
  • Require overlap shifts and a sign-off checklist for new NOC staff before they work solo (onboarding plans).
  • Key personnel (shift leads) are named in the SOW, with notice periods for changes.
  • Run a monthly tabletop where the NOC coordinates. It's practice, and it shows you who is ready.

Shift Health Sweep

Run these in order. Each takes a few seconds. Values highlighted in amber are Nimbus-specific: replace them with your environment's names, or set them once on the query page.

  • 1. Error rate by feature, now vs yesterday
  • 2. P50 / P95 by feature, now vs yesterday
  • 3. Edge (Front Door) status codes
  • 4. 401s at the gateway
  • 5. 429s on Foundry deployments
  • 6. Telemetry volume
  • 7. Local vs cloud routing (device orchestrator)
KQL1 · Error rate by feature, now vs yesterdayApp Insights → Logs
// ── Parameters ─────────────────────────────
let window = 1h;                         // [TUNE] how far back to look
let featureKey = "feature.experience";   // [NIMBUS] custom attribute holding the feature name
// ── Query ──────────────────────────────────
requests
| where timestamp > ago(window) or timestamp between (ago(1d + window) .. ago(1d))
| extend period = iff(timestamp > ago(window), "now", "yesterday")
| extend feature = coalesce(tostring(customDimensions[featureKey]), cloud_RoleName)
| summarize total = sum(itemCount), failed = sumif(itemCount, success == false) by feature, period
| extend error_pct = round(100.0 * failed / total, 2)
| project feature, period, error_pct, total
| order by feature asc, period asc

// ── Parameters ─────────────────────────────

requests

KQL3 · Edge (Front Door) status codesLog Analytics workspace → Logs
// ── Parameters ─────────────────────────────
let window = 1h;                         // [TUNE] how far back to look
let fdCategory = "FrontDoorAccessLog";   // [AZURE] Front Door access log category
// ── Query ──────────────────────────────────
AzureDiagnostics
| where TimeGenerated > ago(window) and Category == fdCategory
| extend status = toint(column_ifexists("httpStatusCode_s", ""))
| summarize requests = count(), edge_5xx = countif(status >= 500), blocked_403 = countif(status == 403)
    by bin(TimeGenerated, 5m)
| extend edge_5xx_pct = round(100.0 * edge_5xx / requests, 2)
| render timechart
KQL4 · 401s at the gateway, by APILog Analytics workspace → Logs
// ── Parameters ─────────────────────────────
let window = 1h;                         // [TUNE] how far back to look
// ── Query ──────────────────────────────────
ApiManagementGatewayLogs
| where TimeGenerated > ago(window)
| summarize requests = count(), unauthorized = countif(ResponseCode == 401) by ApiId
| extend pct_401 = round(100.0 * unauthorized / requests, 2)
| order by pct_401 desc
KQL5 · 429s on Foundry deploymentsApp Insights → Logs
// ── Parameters ─────────────────────────────
let window = 1h;                                                          // [TUNE] how far back to look
let modelHosts = dynamic(["openai.azure.com", "services.ai.azure.com"]);  // [AZURE] Foundry / Azure OpenAI hostnames
// ── Query ──────────────────────────────────
dependencies
| where timestamp > ago(window) and target has_any (modelHosts)
| extend deployment = coalesce(tostring(customDimensions["gen_ai.request.model"]), extract(@"deployments/([^/]+)/", 1, data))
| summarize calls = sum(itemCount), throttled = sumif(itemCount, resultCode == "429") by deployment
| extend throttle_pct = round(100.0 * throttled / calls, 2)
| order by throttle_pct desc
KQL6 · Telemetry volumeApp Insights → Logs · a drop means data loss, not health
// ── Parameters ─────────────────────────────
let window = 2d;                         // [TUNE] long enough to see yesterday's shape
// ── Query ──────────────────────────────────
union requests, dependencies, customEvents
| where timestamp > ago(window)
| summarize items = sum(itemCount) by itemType, bin(timestamp, 15m)
| render timechart
KQL7 · Local vs Cloud Routing by Client VersionApp Insights → Logs · a drop in local answers means a cloud surge is coming
// ── Parameters ─────────────────────────────
let window = 2h;                         // [TUNE] how far back to look
let routingEvent = "routing.decision";   // [NIMBUS] client event emitted by the device orchestrator
let routeKey = "route";                  // [NIMBUS] attribute: "local" or "cloud"
let clientVersionKey = "client.version"; // [NIMBUS] attribute holding the desktop client version
// ── Query ──────────────────────────────────
customEvents
| where timestamp > ago(window) and name == routingEvent
| extend route = tostring(customDimensions[routeKey]), client = tostring(customDimensions[clientVersionKey])
| summarize decisions = sum(itemCount), local = sumif(itemCount, route == "local") by client
| extend local_pct = round(100.0 * local / decisions, 1)
| order by decisions desc
Save the Sweep

Save these as shared queries in the Log Analytics workspace, or pin them to an Azure dashboard. Until Grafana coverage improves, that saved set is the NOC dashboard.

When in doubt, open a channel

Opening an #inc- channel for something that turns out to be nothing costs a minute. Missing a real incident costs far more. Nobody will blame you for being careful.
