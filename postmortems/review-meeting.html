Running the Postmortem Review

The review meeting is where a postmortem either turns into change or turns into blame. Fifty minutes, a clear agenda, and a facilitator who protects the room are enough.

  • Chair: a senior manager for Sev 1–2. Facilitator: anyone trained, ideally not the author.
  • Open by stating the blameless rule. Close with owned, dated actions.
  • Invite every team involved, including Teams-only CFTs and Cloud Infrastructure.

Before the Meeting

  • The draft is shared at least 24 hours ahead, so people come having read it.
  • Calendar invites go to everyone involved. For Teams-only CFTs, also send the invite and doc link in Teams.
  • The chair has personally asked Cloud Infrastructure and the CFT leads to attend if their systems were involved.
  • The timeline is accurate. Ask the NOC Coordinator to confirm the times.

Agenda (50 Minutes)

  1. Blameless opening (2 min)The chair reads it out: "We assume everyone acted reasonably with what they knew. We're here to improve the system, not to find who to blame."
  2. Summary and impact (5 min)The author, in plain language.
  3. Timeline walk-through (15 min)Stop at the key moments. "What did you know here? What made sense at the time?"
  4. Contributing factors (12 min)Look for the conditions behind each decision point, not the person.
  5. Response quality (6 min)Rate each dimension Weak, Good, or Best (rubric), and pick the weakest to improve.
  6. Action items (10 min)Each one gets one owner team, a date, and a type. Write them down live.

Questions That Work

AskInstead of
"What made that seem like the right call at the time?""Why did you do that?"
"What information would have changed the decision?""Didn't you check X?"
"Where did we lose the most time?""Who was slow?"
"What would make this impossible, or obvious, next time?""How do we make sure people are more careful?"
"Where did we get lucky?"(Usually never asked)

Questions by Role

For the NOC Coordinator

When did we declare compared with when impact started? Which requests stalled, and for how long? What interrupted coordination?

For the Technical Lead

How long until we knew the layer? Which telemetry was missing? Which mitigation helped, and could it have come sooner?

For CFTs and Cloud Infrastructure

What did you see from your side? What would have helped you act faster? What change or condition in your system contributed?

For the Chair

Which decisions waited on leadership? Did stakeholder pressure slow the response? What do we need to fund?

Handling Defensiveness

  • "That wasn't our system." "Understood. What would we need to see to rule it in or out? Let's write that down as a detection gap."
  • A powerful team pushing back (often Cloud Infrastructure): keep it on evidence and shared improvements. "What would help your team catch this earlier?" Have the chair agree joint actions with their manager.
  • Someone blaming an individual: the facilitator steps in. "Let's look at what made that easy to do. What conditions were in place?"
  • An executive who dives in again: "Great question for the analysis. Let's capture it as an open question and keep to the timeline."

After the Meeting

  • The author updates the doc with the agreed factors and actions within 1 business day.
  • Action items are created and linked to the incident ticket (how).
  • The summary is shared with all involved teams, in Slack and Teams.
  • The playbook or runbook is updated with what we learned.
