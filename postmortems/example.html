Worked Example: Device Care 429s

A complete, fictional, blameless postmortem showing what "Good" looks like. It involves several contributing factors, two teams on different chat tools, a powerful platform team, and a director who dove in.

Severity: Sev 2 · Date: 2026-09-21 · Duration: 1 h 25 m (09:02–10:27 local) · Coordinator: NOC (Priya) · Technical Lead: SRE (Jordan) · Chair: Senior Manager · Status: Reviewed

Summary

For about 85 minutes, Device Care requests failed at up to 14% because the shared chat-small model deployment ran out of capacity. Two things combined: the daily Proactive Tips batch, and a surge of requests from devices whose new on-device model (v2.3) was failing and falling back to the cloud. Service recovered after the batch was paused and the on-device model rollout was halted.

User Impact

  • Device Care: error rate peaked at 14% (baseline 0.6%), and P95 rose from 9 s to 21 s. About 38,000 users saw "Nimbus is busy".
  • Quick on-device tasks were slower for about 12% of devices (the v2.3 cohort).
  • Proactive Tips were delayed by up to 3 hours. This was acceptable.

Detection

The NOC's 09:00 shift sweep flagged 429s on chat-small at 09:11, 9 minutes after impact began. No alert fired, because no alert existed for quota saturation or fallback rate.

Contributing Factors

  • Shared capacity: interactive Device Care and background Proactive Tips share chat-small, with no priority between them.
  • Fallback isn't a rollout gate: the on-device model rollout checked crash rate but not fallback rate, so v2.3's failures shifted load to the cloud silently.
  • No saturation or fallback alerts: detection depended on the NOC's sweep timing.
  • Request latency: the NOC can't act directly, and the pause request took 17 minutes, partly because the Insights CFT's on-call is business hours only.
  • Split visibility: Cloud Infrastructure saw "more traffic, no errors in our layer", and the Client CFT saw "rollout healthy". Neither saw the whole picture until the Technical Lead joined the data.
  • Stakeholder interruptions: with no exec update format and no comms buffer named at the start, a director's questions went straight to the Technical Lead during diagnosis.

What Went Well

  • The NOC declared within 9 minutes of impact and ran a clear channel with a Teams bridge.
  • The Technical Lead used the token-share and fallback queries to prove the cause with evidence.
  • The senior manager approved the spillover cost within 4 minutes.

What Was Hard

  • Two causes at once: pausing the batch helped, but it hid the second cause for a while.
  • Cloud Infrastructure needed evidence before accepting that capacity was the problem.

Where We Got Lucky

  • The v2.3 rollout was at 25%, not 100%.
  • It happened during business hours, when the Insights CFT was online.
