Postmortems

An incident you don't learn from is one you'll have again. Postmortems are one of our weakest practices: they get skipped, started and abandoned, or written as a blame document, and their action items disappear. This track makes them routine, blameless, and useful.

  • Required for every Sev 1 and Sev 2, every repeat incident, and any incident where customers told us first.
  • Timeline: scheduled before stand-down, draft in 2 business days, review within 5, and action items reviewed monthly until closed.
  • Roles: the NOC brings the timeline, SRE writes the analysis, CFTs own the fixes, and a senior manager chairs and funds.
  • Blameless: ask how the system made the failure possible, not who caused it.
Applies Anywhere

A postmortem is worth what its action items change. A beautiful document with no completed actions is the same as no postmortem. Measure postmortems by follow-through, not by page count.

When a Postmortem Is Required

  • Every Sev 1 and Sev 2.
  • Any repeat of an incident in the last 90 days.
  • Any incident where customers or executives told us first.
  • Any incident where escalation stalled for 30+ minutes.
  • Anything a responder asks for. Near misses teach a lot too.

The Timeline

  1. At stand-down (NOC)Schedule the review within 5 business days, assign the author, and hand over the timeline from the pinned record.
  2. Within 2 business days (SRE author)Draft using the template and writer. Gather input from the CFTs and Cloud Infrastructure.
  3. Within 5 business days (manager chairs)Hold the blameless review meeting and agree action items with owners and dates.
  4. Within 1 week after reviewAction items are in Jira (or the tracking sheet) and linked to the incident ticket.
  5. MonthlyReview open action items at the operations review until every one is closed or explicitly dropped.

Who Does What

RolePostmortem responsibility
NOC (Coordinator)Schedules the review, provides the timeline and decision log, and records escalation and response times.
SRE (Technical Lead)Writes the draft: technical analysis, contributing factors, and proposed actions.
CFTs and Cloud InfrastructureContribute their view, own their action items, and attend the review.
Senior ManagerChairs Sev 1–2 reviews, protects blamelessness, funds actions, and runs the monthly follow-up.

Quality Rubric: Weak / Good / Best

Weak

Late or never written. A single "root cause", often a person. Vague actions with no owner ("be more careful"). Nobody reads it after the meeting.

Good

Written within days, with a clear timeline and impact, blameless contributing factors, and 3–7 specific owned actions with dates. Reviewed with every team involved.

Best in Class

Good, plus actions change the system (gates, alerts, architecture), follow-through is tracked to 100%, learnings are shared across teams, and repeat incidents become rare.
