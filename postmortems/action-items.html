Action Items That Get Done

Action items are the whole point of a postmortem. Most teams write too many, write them vaguely, and never check whether they were done. Here's how to write fewer, better ones and see them through, even with a locked-down Jira.

  • 3–7 actions per postmortem. Each one is specific, has one owner team, a due date, and a type.
  • Prefer actions that change the system over actions that ask people to try harder.
  • Track them linked to the incident ticket, and review open ones monthly until closed or explicitly dropped.

Writing Good Action Items

"Be more careful with deploys.""Client CFT: add fallback rate to the rollout health gate, auto-halting above 5%. Due Nov 15. Type: prevent."
"Improve monitoring.""Cloud Infrastructure: alert when chat-small tokens per minute exceed 80% of quota for 10 min. Due Nov 1. Type: detect."

Action Types

Prevent

Stop it happening: gates, limits, separation, tests.

Detect

Notice sooner: alerts, dashboards, telemetry.

Mitigate

Recover faster: runbooks, tooling, failover.

Process

Coordinate better: roles, paging, communication.

Tracking With a Locked-Down Jira

  1. One incident ticketFiled by the NOC after the incident, in the existing project (how).
  2. Each action as a normal taskCreated in the owner team's project if possible, linked to the incident ticket and labelled postmortem-action.
  3. If you can't create tasks in their projectAsk the owner team to create it during the review, and paste the link into the postmortem.
  4. A simple tracking listKeep a shared list (or a filter on the label) of all open postmortem actions: incident, action, owner, due, status.

The Monthly Review

At the monthly operations review, the senior manager walks the open-actions list:

  • Overdue? Ask what's blocking it, then unblock it, fund it, or explicitly drop it with a reason.
  • Done? Ask how we'll know it worked.
  • Repeat incident? Check whether an earlier action would have prevented it.
