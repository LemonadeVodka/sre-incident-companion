Planning & Capacity

SRE teams can't plan like product teams. Pages, incidents, NOC requests, and director asks arrive every week. Good planning admits that up front: it reserves room for interrupts, protects time for the work that reduces them, and uses data to adjust the split each cycle.

  • Start with a split like 50% projects, 30% KTLO and toil, 20% interrupts, then tune it from your labels.
  • Subtract on-call first. The person on call isn't project capacity.
  • Error budget burned → reliability work goes first. That's the deal with product teams.
  • Every unplanned item that enters the cycle displaces something, so name the trade-off.

The Capacity Split

BucketStarting pointIncludesAdjust when
Projects~50%Roadmap work: automation, observability, resilience, NOC toolingProjects keep slipping, so check whether interrupts are eating them
KTLO & Toil~30%Upgrades, certificates, quota requests, manual operationsToil grows above about 50% of anyone's time. Google SRE's guidance is to cap it there.
Interrupt Buffer~20%Incident follow-ups, urgent NOC and CFT asks, director requestsThe buffer is used up every cycle (grow it) or never (shrink it)

Measure the real split from your work-type labels each cycle, and adjust. The point isn't the exact numbers. It's making the trade-off visible.

Cycle Planning Ritual (45 Minutes)

  1. Look Back (10 min)What finished, what carried over and why, and the real split vs the plan.
  2. Capacity (5 min)People × days, minus on-call, leave, holidays in both countries, and training. Plan to about 80% of what's left.
  3. Must-Dos (10 min)High-priority incident actions, KTLO with deadlines (certificates, retirements, quota), and commitments to other teams.
  4. Projects (15 min)Pull the next ready items from the projects, by priority, until capacity is full.
  5. Risks (5 min)Dependencies, and anything likely to slip. Add them to the RAID log.
Holidays in Two Countries

Keep a shared calendar of US and Indian public holidays (including regional ones) and festival seasons. They change capacity and on-call coverage more than people expect.

Prioritization

AskWhy
"Which SLO or user problem does this improve?"Ties work to reliability outcomes
"What happens if we don't do it this quarter?"Finds the real urgency
"How much toil or how many pages does it remove?"Compounding returns. Automation frees future capacity.
"How big is it, honestly?"Impact ÷ effort. Do the cheap high-impact items first.
"Is the error budget burned for this feature?"If yes, reliability work moves ahead of features (error budgets)

Roadmap and OKRs

Keep the SRE roadmap to three to five outcomes a quarter, each written as a measurable result rather than a task:

Task-Shaped

"Build Grafana dashboards." "Automate restarts." "Write runbooks."

Outcome-Shaped

"Every Nimbus feature has a P50/P95 and error-rate dashboard the NOC uses daily." "NOC-detected incidents rise from 40% to 70%." "Toil drops below 30% for both regions."

Handling Unplanned Work

  1. Label Ittype:interrupt, plus where it came from.
  2. Size ItIs it an hour, or a week?
  3. Name the Trade-Off"If we take this, [item] moves to next cycle." Say it to the requester, including your director.
  4. DecideTake it now, schedule it next cycle, or decline with options (template).

Don't Use Flow Metrics on People

These measure the system, not individuals. Comparing people's throughput encourages small, safe tickets and hides hard work. Keep them at team level.
