Risks & Dependencies

With centralized SREs and a powerful Cloud Infrastructure team, much of your work depends on other teams. Projects rarely fail on the work you control. They fail on the dependency nobody chased. This play makes risks visible early and escalates them before they bite.

  • Keep a RAID log per project, and review it weekly.
  • Every dependency gets a named person, a date, and a Linear issue, even if the other team uses Jira.
  • Escalate on a trigger you agreed in advance, not when you're frustrated.

The RAID Log

LetterMeansNimbus example
RisksThings that might happen and hurt delivery"Cloud Infrastructure may not approve the secondary-region quota before launch."
AssumptionsThings we're treating as true but haven't confirmed"The Insights CFT will accept a 30-minute delay on Next Step."
IssuesRisks that have happened"The model version retirement date moved forward by a month."
DependenciesWork another team must deliver"The Client CFT must ship trace-context propagation in client version X."

For each entry: owner, likelihood and impact (for risks), next action, and date. Review it in the weekly project update.

Asking for a Dependency

  1. Find the Right DoorTheir intake process (Jira project, channel, or form), not a DM to a friendly engineer.
  2. Make It Easy to Say YesWhat you need, why (user or SLO impact), by when, and the smallest version that would help.
  3. Track It on Your BoardOne Linear issue, linked to their ticket, with the blocked label until it moves.
  4. Follow Up on a ScheduleAgree a date. If it passes, follow up once, then escalate.

Use the dependency request template.

When to Escalate

TriggerAction
No acknowledgement after 3 working daysEngineer follows up once, in their channel
No progress by the agreed dateYou contact their manager, peer to peer
It now threatens a launch, an SLO, or a commitment to executivesJoint escalation to the shared leader, with options (template)

How to escalate without burning the relationship is on Escalation & Narrative. Build credit first with the political capital habits.

Reporting Status Upward

  • Green / amber / red with a reason, not just a colour. "Amber: quota approval pending, decision by Oct 20."
  • Report red early. Bad news gets worse with age, and a highly technical director will find it anyway.
  • Pair every red with an ask: a decision, an escalation, or a scope cut.
  • Roll project updates into your weekly note, using the project update template.
