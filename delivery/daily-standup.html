The SRE Daily

Fifteen minutes, once a day, in the overlap between the US and India. Done well, it keeps work flowing, surfaces blockers early, and lets you coach through questions. Done badly, it's a status report to you that everyone resents. This page is how to do it well with your TPM hat on.

  • The board is the status report. Update Linear before the call, and spend the call on exceptions.
  • Walk the board right to left. Finish work before starting new work.
  • PULSE anything that's stuck: Priority, Unblock, Load, Scope, ETA.
  • Problem-solving goes to the parking lot, straight after the call, with only the people needed.

The 15-Minute Format

  1. Interrupts (2 min)Incidents, pages, and NOC escalations in the last 24 hours, and anything still live. Who's on call now?
  2. Walk the Board (10 min)Right to left: Done → In Review → In Progress → Todo. Spend time only on items that are stuck, aging, or at risk.
  3. Blocked (2 min)Every blocked item leaves with an owner, a next step, and a date, including escalations you'll take on.
  4. Parking Lot (after)Topics that need discussion. Only the people needed stay. Everyone else gets their time back.

Walk the Board, Right to Left

Starting at the right of the Linear board (closest to done) focuses everyone on finishing. Work that's 90% done delivers nothing until it ships.

The PULSE Framework

For any item that's stuck, aging, or important, run a quick PULSE. You don't need all five every time. Pick the one that matters.

Questions by Person

Junior SRE

Coach, don't rescue. "How would you find out?" "What have you tried?" Wait for the answer. If they're stuck, offer the next rung, not the solution. Follow up the next day: "What did you find?"

Quiet Engineer

Invite them by name with an open question before the senior voices speak. Alternate which region goes first. Praise good blockers raised early.

Senior SRE

Challenge: "What would make this fail?" "What's the simpler version?" "Who could you pair with so this isn't only in your head?"

Ops Lead

Your TPM apprentice. Have them facilitate once a week, then debrief privately on which questions moved things. See their plan.

Running It Across the US and India

  • Protect the overlap. The daily is the only meeting that must be in the overlap. Keep everything else async.
  • Share the pain. If the overlap is early for one region and late for the other, alternate who stretches, monthly.
  • Update before the call. Each person updates their Linear issues (or posts a one-line comment) before joining.
  • Rotate the facilitator between regions, so India isn't always reporting to the US.
  • Hand off through Linear, not memory: decisions and next steps go in the issue during the call. See Follow the Sun.
  • Camera optional, focus required. Keep it to 15 minutes. People are at the start or end of their day.

Manager Anti-Patterns

Status Theater

Everyone recites yesterday and today to you. Replace it with the board walk.

Solving in the Standup

A 10-minute debugging session for two people while eight wait. Send it to the parking lot.

Asking What the Board Says

If Linear already shows it, don't ask. Ask about what the board can't tell you.

Director Hijack

A director's ask arrives and the whole team pivots. Take it offline and treat it as unplanned work with an explicit trade-off.

Public Pressure

"Why is this still not done?" in front of the group. Ask PULSE questions instead, and keep performance conversations for 1:1s.

Weak / Good / Best

Weak

A 40-minute round robin. Linear is stale. Blockers are mentioned every day with no action. The manager does most of the talking.

Good

Fifteen minutes. The board is updated beforehand. Right-to-left walk. Blockers leave with an owner and a date. The parking lot is used.

Best

Facilitation rotates across regions. The team asks PULSE questions of each other. Aging work is rare, and the manager mostly listens.
