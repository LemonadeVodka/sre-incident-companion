Incidents and Reporting

Executive SITREP

Use during any Sev 1–2 for executives. Bottom line first. See Executive Updates, or build one with the SITREP builder.

MessageSITREP
[SEV X] [Feature] [degraded/down] — Update #[n] — [time, timezone]

BOTTOM LINE: [One sentence: what users experience and whether it's getting better or worse.]

IMPACT: [Who and how many] · [which features] · since [start time]
CAUSE: [Known / suspected / unknown]: [one line, plain language]
ACTIONS: [What we're doing now] — owner [team]
DECISIONS NEEDED: [None / the specific decision, from whom, by when]
NEXT UPDATE: [time], or sooner if anything changes

Weekly Reliability Note

Five lines to your director and peers every week. Consistency builds trust. See Reporting Cadence.

MessageWeekly Note
Reliability — week of [date]

1. SLOs: [n of m features met SLO]. Misses: [feature — P95 / error rate vs target].
2. Incidents: [count by sev]. Notable: [one line each, with postmortem link].
3. Trend to watch: [e.g. model TPM at 82% of quota at peak, up from 70%].
4. Progress: [postmortem actions closed / open / overdue] · [one win from the team, by name].
5. Ask / heads-up: [decision or help needed, or "none"].

Quarterly Reliability Review

The executive narrative. Five slides or one page.

OutlineQuarterly Review
1. Bottom line: Is Nimbus reliable enough for our users and our growth plan? [Yes / not yet, because...]
2. Trends: SLO attainment per feature, incidents by severity, time to mitigate (quarter over quarter)
3. Top 3 incidents and what we changed because of them
4. Risks ahead: capacity outlook (peak vs quota), model retirements, launches, partner changes
5. Investments needed: [ask, cost, risk if not funded] — link the decision memo
6. Team: wins, recognition by name, staffing and on-call health

Expectations Brief to the Vendor

Addressed to the vendor's delivery manager, not to individual contractors. Set outcomes and let the vendor manage its people. Confirm the wording with HR and procurement. See Co-Employment Boundaries.

EmailExpectations Brief
Subject: NOC service expectations for [quarter] — per SOW [ref]

Hi [Delivery Manager],

Ahead of [quarter], here are the outcomes we need from the NOC service, aligned to the SOW:

1. Detection: [target], measured on the monthly scorecard.
2. Declaration: use the Triage or Incident framework; declare within [15] minutes when criteria are met.
3. Coordination: run the IC loop and post SITREPs on the agreed cadence for Sev 1–2.
4. Knowledge: each shift contributes [n] runbook improvements per month.
5. Training: complete [modules] by [date]; please share your plan for staffing it.

Where we see gaps, we'll raise them with you in our weekly sync and the monthly scorecard,
and rely on you to manage staffing, coaching, and performance within your team.

Thanks — [Name]

SBI Feedback

Situation, Behavior, Impact, then a question. Works downward, sideways, and upward. See SBI.

ScriptSBI
"In [situation — when and where],
when you [specific, observable behavior],
the impact was [effect on people, outcome, or trust].
[For positive feedback: "Please keep doing that."]
[For constructive feedback: "What was going on from your side?" — then listen.]
Next time, could we try [specific alternative]?"

One page to get a decision. See Decision Memo for a worked example.

DocumentDecision Memo
ASK: [Decision needed] from [who] by [date].

CONTEXT: [Problem in three sentences, with data: user impact, incidents, cost.]

OPTIONS:
 A. [Option] — cost [ ], time [ ], risk [ ], benefit [ ]
 B. [Option] — cost [ ], time [ ], risk [ ], benefit [ ]
 C. Do nothing — cost of inaction [ ]

RECOMMENDATION: [Option] because [two sentences].

RISKS AND MITIGATIONS: [ ]

ALIGNED: [Who you pre-wired and their position.]

Pre-Wire Message

Before an important meeting, so nobody is surprised. See Pre-Wiring.

MessagePre-Wire
Hi [Name] — before [meeting] on [date], I wanted to get your view on [topic].

The short version: [recommendation in one sentence].
Why it matters to you: [their goal or pain — e.g. fewer throttling incidents on your feature].
What I'd need from you: [support / input / nothing, just no surprises].

Is there anything you'd change, or concerns I should address before we're in the room?

Technical Pre-Read for Your Director

Send 24 hours before a review, so a highly technical director can dig in privately rather than in front of the team. See Managing Up.

DocumentPre-Read
Pre-read: [Topic / incident] — review on [date]

Bottom line: [one sentence]
Data: [links to dashboards / queries, with time range]

What we don't know yet: [ ]

Proposed actions: [ ]
Questions for you: [where your expertise would help most]

To keep the review productive for the team, please send technical questions ahead
and I'll make sure we bring the answers.

Escalation Email

Escalate the problem, not the person. See Escalation & Narrative.

EmailEscalation
Subject: Decision needed: [issue] — by [date]

Hi [Leader] (cc [peer you've already spoken to]),

[Peer] and I have been working on [issue] and need your help to resolve it.

Impact: [user / business impact, with data].
What we've tried: [steps, dates].
Where we disagree: [the trade-off, stated fairly from both sides].
Options: A [ ] · B [ ]. My recommendation: [ ]. [Peer] prefers [ ] because [ ].
Decision needed by: [date], because [reason].

Happy to walk through it in 15 minutes.

"No, but Here Are Options"

Decline a request without being the blocker. See Escalation & Narrative.

MessageNo, with Options
"I want to help with [request]. With current capacity we can't do it and also
[current commitment] by [date]. Here are the options:

1. Do [request] now — [commitment] slips to [date].
2. Do a smaller version: [scope] by [date], keep [commitment].
3. Do [request] after [date].

My recommendation is [option] because [reason]. Which would you prefer?"
